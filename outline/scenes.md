# Scenes

The scene maps of the book: the step between **Chapters** and **Book**. For each chapter, the events listed in Chapters are expanded into ordered scenes with everything a writer needs: when, where, point of view, characters, what happens, motivations, feelings, place details, history carried, function in the chapter, voice, open items and target length. Built by the **scene-builder** skill; written as prose by the **co-writer** in the planned voices.

**The process**: 00 Book Concept → Chapters → **Scenes** → Book. The Concept branches into the reference files used at every level: the Character Bible (with its Continuity section), the historical research (Registros historicos) and the Voices.

**Rules**: every event in a chapter's Chapters entry appears in at least one scene; nothing appears here that is not in Chapters or the Character Bible unless marked **\[proposal\]**; no new named characters without Rafael's approval; facts that must be accurate carry a source or a truth label. Approved proposals are written back into Chapters. Every scene also carries a Reader feeling field (added Oct 10, 2026): the emotion the reader should feel, not only what the characters feel, and the sensory details that create it.

## Chapter 1 — Good Teeth (scene map, draft 2, Oct 10, 2026)

Source in Chapters: ch. 1 (revised Oct 10, 2026). Voice: NARR-MAIN (close third, to be defined), following Luzia. Target: about 5,500 words. Throughline: two blows of violence open the chapter (scenes 1 and 2); from scene 4 one question drives it, will the child be Black or pardo, answered only in the last scene. Status: scenes 1 and 2 revised; scenes 3 to 9 to revise one by one.

### Scene 1 — Good teeth

  - When: c. 1832–33, a few days after a clandestine night landing. Luzia about 14.
  - Where: Salvador, Cidade Baixa: a back room of a warehouse near the port. After the 1831 ban, smuggled Africans were sold quietly in depots and private houses (PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION, to verify).
  - POV: Luzia, from inside her silence. She does not understand Portuguese yet; the words are noise, the hands are clear.
  - Characters: Luzia; two buyers, the dealer, other captives from the same ship (all unnamed).
  - Beats: the captives are washed with vinegar and lined up. A buyer opens her mouth and runs a thumb along her teeth; his hands stay too long and his eyes go where a buyer's have no need to go. She does not fully understand it; she keeps her face still and her eyes on a thread of light in the planks. Her age is guessed aloud, the price chalked on a board, haggled as over a horse. She is sold and led out into the light: the city climbing the cliff, the bay below.
  - Motivations: the buyers want a young body that will last; the dealer wants a fast sale before questions are asked; Luzia does not fully understand how she feels, she is tired from the trip and wants it to be over. She is removed from the pack because she is beautiful and her value is estimated differently. They decide to clean and dress her differently. She needs to be untouched to be sold for a better price.
  - Reader feeling: disgust. The buyer is a dirty old man, and the sexual way he handles her is repellent; she does not yet understand it, the reader does. Everything stinks: the room of tar, salt fish, sweat and vinegar; the men are dirty, their breath stinks of tobacco and rum when they lean close. The reader should want to wash. Underneath, a held breath of fear.
  - History carried: by the law of 7 November 1831 she is free; she is sold anyway. Never stated: the night landing on a beach, the dealer's hurry, nothing written down (FICTIONAL details on a DOCUMENTED background; see Chapters ch. 1).
  - Forces: money (a price on a body), violence, luck (which buyer takes her).
  - Function: the hook. Plants the look she will learn to recognise in men like Coronel Menezes (pays off in scene 6); the teeth check is echoed in ch. 5.
  - Target: about 800 words.

### Scene 2 — The square (merges the trial's scenes 2 and 3)

  - When: Salvador, early 1835, a few weeks after the Malê Revolt. Luzia about 16, two years in the city.
  - Where: a ladeira down to the waterfront market, then a square near the Rosário church.
  - POV: Luzia.
  - Characters: Luzia; her owner, the widow (seen only when Luzia pays the jornal); a junta woman; a Malê cloth seller she knows from the market; soldiers; the crowd (all unnamed).
  - Beats: (1) She comes down the ladeira under the tray, singing; the street stops to listen; half the tray is sold before the bottom. (2) She knots the jornal into her cloth and spends the rest at once: a coin to the old carrier who can no longer work, bread for a sick child's mother. Her money is shown through her hands, never explained. (3) The junta woman walks beside her and offers her a place in the savings circle; Luzia laughs without slowing: what the orixás want will come. (4) A drum, shouting; soldiers push everyone into the square; she is shoved to the front. (5) The cloth seller is tied to the post; the leather amulet with Arabic writing he once offered her is held up as proof; she counts the lashes the way she counts coins and loses count. (6) A soldier studies her face; she empties it. Nobody teaches her; she learns it there. (7) His eyes find hers: the street's half-freedom is gone; she is a body that can be tied to a post. (8) Hook: that evening she pays the widow the full jornal, then gives every remaining coin to the cloth seller's wife.
  - Motivations: Luzia: sell, help others, stay unseen. The junta woman: a practical road to freedom.
  - Reader feeling: ease and warmth first (her voice, the street stopping to listen, her open hands); then shock and helpless anger at the whipping, with the smells of sweat, dust and blood and the crush of bodies; at the end, a tender ache as she gives her last coins away.
  - History carried: the Malê Revolt (24–25 January 1835) and the public floggings after it; the jornal; the juntas (DOCUMENTED background; details PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION; source to add: João José Reis, Rebelião escrava no Brasil).
  - Forces: violence, money, luck as faith.
  - Function: the first of the two moments that teach her what she is (the second is scene 6); her generosity and faith in luck shown in action.
  - Target: about 700 words.
