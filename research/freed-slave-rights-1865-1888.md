# Research — What a Freed Slave Could and Couldn't Do (1865–1888)

Oct 2, 2026 · Rafael

Research base for the chapter **"Free, not equal"** (1870–c. 1875, age 24–29, Corumbá). Freedom in Imperial Brazil was real but fragile: there was no law reserving parts of a city for Black people. The limits came through papers, police, work, politics and the constant suspicion of being a slave.

Status labels: DOCUMENTED (we have a source) · TO VERIFY (plausible, source still needed).

## 1. The rules

| **Area** | **What it meant for a freedman** | **Status** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Proving freedom | Courts and police presumed people of African descent were slaves; the burden of proof was on them. Free people arrested "on suspicion" could be auctioned if no owner claimed them in time. In practice he must carry his carta de alforria everywhere. | DOCUMENTED | Chalhoub, A força da escravidão (2012) — review: <https://www.cecult.ifch.unicamp.br/pf-cecult/public-files/publicacoes/8172/resenha-forca-escravidao-beatriz-mamigonian.pdf> |
| Freedom could be revoked | Until 1871 a former master could revoke a manumission for "ingratitude" (Ordenações Filipinas, book 4, title 63). Ended by the Free Womb Law, 28 Sept 1871, art. 4 §9. | DOCUMENTED | Lei 2.040/1871: <https://www.legjur.com/legislacao/htm/lei_00020401871> |

## 2. Corumbá in the 1870s (setting for the chapter)

  - River port boom after 1870: the Paraguay River opened to international navigation and imperial tax incentives turned Corumbá into the province's main trading port. Source: <https://periodicos.ufpb.br/index.php/srh/article/download/16430/9418/28317>
  - About 3,086 inhabitants in 1872, roughly 21% foreigners; between 1870 and 1880 most immigrants were Paraguayans (859 of 1,276 arrivals in 1876), many working as farmhands and cattle hands. Same source.
  - 275 enslaved people in the parish of Corumbá in 1872 — slavery present but small. Source: <https://ojs.ufgd.edu.br/entre-lugar/article/download/10207/5582/35116>
  - Ladário naval arsenal nearby employed hundreds until post-war budget cuts.
  - Jacadigo (future farms): 30–40 km from Corumbá, Bolivian border, near the Lagoa do Jacadigo and the Urucum hills. Source: Região doc (Drive).

## 3. Story seeds for "Free, not equal"

1.  The paper — his carta de alforria sewn into his jacket. One night he's stopped without it and spends a night in the Corumbá jail, where a clerk mentions an auction. Someone vouches for him; from then on he is afraid of losing a sheet of paper.
2.  The land that never comes — he keeps returning to the military office about the plot the 1865 decree promised. A white comrade's claim goes through; his stays "under review". The clerk asks for one more document each time, or spells his name differently on every form. No one ever says why.
3.  Working beside the enemy — porter, ranch hand or arsenal labourer next to Paraguayan immigrants, the men he fought. They share the worst jobs.
4.  The old master's letter — a letter from Bahia hints that his freedom could be revoked for "ingratitude". The threat hangs over him until September 1871, when he hears the Free Womb Law read aloud and knows it can no longer be taken.
5.  The vote that isn't his — a local boss herds him to vote in the parish election for a man who will never answer to him.
6.  The vagrancy threat — between jobs, a policeman warns him that a man with no work is a vagrant.
7.  The ticket — with his last coins he buys a lottery ticket. Hook into the Luck chapter.
