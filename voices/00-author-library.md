## Portraits

  - **Ana Maria Gonçalves**: an old, nearly blind woman tells her life to the son she lost; warm first-person testimony, densely researched (Malê revolt, markets, rituals), history through lived experience. Clear about the wrongs of slavery (clashes with the no-morals rule). Read: opening chapters (childhood, slave-ship crossing). Suits: letters, grandma frame.
  - **Itamar Vieira Junior**: three narrators in turn (two sisters, then an encantada spirit) on a farm in the Bahia backlands; musical prose rooted in the land; history implicit. Read: chapter 1 (the knife), opening of Part III. Suits: main narration (lyrical).
  - **Gabriel García Márquez**: all-seeing narrator telling generations as legend, the impossible told deadpan (he credited his grandmother's way of telling); fate and cycles; wars in summary. Read: chapter 1, the banana-company massacre (ch. 15). Suits: grandma frame, main narration.
  - **Augusto Roa Bastos**: Paraguayan history from inside, Francia to the Chaco War; mixed narrators, Guaraní myth with brutal realism; morally ambiguous Miguel Vera. Read: Hijo de hombre, chapter "Sed". Suits: main narration, legends, the Paraguayan side.
  - **David Szalay**: things happen to the protagonist by chance; bare sentences, one-word dialogue, years skipped, zero commentary. Read: Flesh, chapter 1. Suits: main narration (sparse), contrast.
  - **Cormac McCarthy**: frontier scalp hunters, 1849; long biblical sentences, archaic words, explicit violence with no moral comment. Read: chapter 1, the Comanche attack on Captain White's company. Suits: war scenes. Hardest to carry into Portuguese.
  - **Svetlana Alexievich**: a chorus of real women's war testimony; broken sentences, small physical memories, raw emotion, the author only arranges. Read: any group of monologues, the author's opening notes. Suits: letters from women in desperate conditions (decided), documents and voices, grandma frame.
