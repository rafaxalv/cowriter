### 17\. The Name

  - When / where: 1888–1890s · Jacadigo
  - Readiness: 52/100 · beats 2 · arc 3 · links 3 · facts 2 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **1888. The gates** (moved from the old ch. 16, decided Nov 2, 2026): one small event, not a long scene, that adds to the decay of his mind and understanding. The peak of his paranoia. Fugitives from nearby ranches arrive at his gates, and he takes them in. For a while he hosts them: a Black landowner sheltering enslaved people on the run, a strange and rich situation. They work his land, eat at his table, hide when the ranchers' men come asking. He is proud, generous, the master of his house, and it is new for him. It does not last. He starts to suspect them: they want to steal from him, take advantage of him. And he is jealous: one of the fugitives looks at his wife, or he believes he does. In the end he sends them all away in anger. The man who was enslaved shuts his gates on people running from slavery.
      - Abolition (Lei Áurea, 1888) and the Republic (1889). He celebrates; his white neighbours never accept him.
      - **Bastião's death** (decided Oct 30, 2026; how, decided Nov 1, 2026). In the 1890s the Pires' men steal some of Liberdade's cattle, and Bastião finds out. He is killed defending Liberdade. Benedito finds him and buries him himself: a very sad moment, the loss of the oldest friend he has (possible echo: Luzia's lament over Inácio, ch. 2). He cannot prove it was the Pires' men. He accuses the Pires; they shrug and say he is going crazy. He is gaslighted and gets very angry. His family, more interested in keeping good relations with the Pires, force him to drop the fight. He resents it until the day he dies; it adds to his frustration, his loneliness and the confusion in his head, and in the end his whole family becomes part of the gaslighting. It is the last important loss before his death, probably the event that sets him on the path to losing his sanity and dying.
      - His last years. As he grows old his memory starts to fail, and the Pires and his own family sometimes use that to gaslight him. Only now do the headaches from Humaitá become a real problem (decided Nov 1, 2026): they add to his confusion and make him even angrier. No disease is ever named; the reader may connect the blow of 1868 with his decline, but the text never says so. He starts doubting even himself. Never call it dementia or name any disease: even the reader should be confused. Is his memory wrong? Are the Pires lying? Is his wife doubting him for no reason? (Echo of grandma's fog in the prologue.)
      - One of his sons hums one of Luzia's songs without knowing what it is.
      - All the loyal employees and ex war veterans are now dead (the slow loss began in ch. 15). The new employees dont care as much, dont work hard as much. The practices that made the farm competitive get lost over time as the old employees die. The new ones are not interested in learning. Competition is stronger and the farm starts loosing money.
      - **Alone** (decided Oct 30, 2026). By his last years every trusted friend is gone: Bastião, Firmino, the veterans of Liberdade; from Bahia, no news of Tonico ever again. He dies feeling alone, even with his wife and children around him (his sons, he still feels, were never his friends, ch. 16): they are there, but none of them knew the boy, the soldier or the songs.
      - His death. He tells his wife his true name; the reader never hears it. She keeps it. Her grief is real (decided Nov 1, 2026): beneath everything she loved him in her way, and it surprises her. Around him, the lament comes back, in the sons' mouths or only in his head: "We look for our father, and cannot find him." The watercourse keeps its name (see research/music/luzia-songs.md).
