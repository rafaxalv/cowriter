### 8\. Asunción

  - When / where: 1 Jan – Jul 1869, age 22–23 · occupied Asunción: the sacked houses, the army hospital, the camps around the city
  - Readiness: 74/100 · beats 4 · arc 4 · links 4 · facts 4 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 3, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **How the chapter runs** (revised Nov 3, 2026). The empty city and the sacking → the hospital: the wounded Paraguayan boys, Ceferina → "the war is over": hope comes back, and the book → Ramon Benites: he tries and fails → months of waiting → the Count d'Eu restarts the hunt, and he is sent back to the line. No dates in the text beyond the entry into the city; the months of waiting blur (see Dates and time, Part II). The breath between two wounds (Humaitá, ch. 7; Acosta Ñu, ch. 9), and the only time in the war when it seems to be over. **His arc: hope, then taken** (decided Nov 3, 2026): after giving up at Humaitá he lets himself hope again, and the hope is taken away, so the fall into ch. 9 is steeper.
      - **1. Entering an empty Asunción (1 Jan 1869)** (decided Oct 30, 2026; beats Nov 3, 2026). He enters with the troops, his first time back with a rifle since the blow at Humaitá, the headaches still with him. Beats: the silent streets, doors open, a city emptied two days before; the sacking starts the moment the troops arrive: palaces, the López family's houses, the Congress stripped of pianos, mirrors, carpets, paintings and silver, carried down to the ships for Rio and Buenos Aires; a man who was property walks through the empty houses of the rich while everyone takes what they want; in a room of books he takes one, a Spanish *Don Quixote* (decided Nov 3, 2026), small enough for his pack. He is learning to read (Dom Obá's newspapers, ch. 4); while the others carry off pianos and silver, his loot is quiet and private. What it leaves: the first thing he owns that he chose himself. (Set-up: the book stays with him for years; Carmen finds Sancho's line in it, ch. 15.)
      - **2. The hospital: the Paraguayan boys** (decided Nov 3, 2026). The wounded of the Dezembrada (Dec 1868) are brought to Asunción, and he is back at the healer's work (ch. 6). Among them are Paraguayan boys from Avaí (11 Dec 1868). He treats them with Luzia's methods, the same as his own men. The boys in three steps: water for the starving boy prisoner at Humaitá (ch. 7) → nursing the wounded boys here → the boy he kills at Acosta Ñu (ch. 9).
      - **3. Ceferina** (decided Nov 1, 2026; encounter Nov 3, 2026). A *residenta*, one of the hungry women coming back to the city, comes to the hospital looking for her son among the wounded boys from Avaí. He is not there. Benedito gives her his ration, and she is gone. Only a very short passage, a random encounter that happens and passes, with no love and no debt to resolve. Echo, never pointed out: a mother searching for her son, as Luzia and he search the lists for each other (ch. 10, 13).
      - **4. "The war is over": hope comes back** (decided Nov 3, 2026). Beats: Caxias enters the city (5 Jan) and declares the war over; the camp believes it is going home; Caxias faints at mass (17 Jan) and leaves (19 Jan), and nobody takes his place for months. At night he reads the *Quixote*, word by word, a Spanish he half understands from prisoners and the women in the camps; he lets himself think of Bahia and of finding Luzia. The first hope since he gave up at Humaitá (ch. 7): luck may not have abandoned him after all.
      - **5. Ramon Benites: he tries and fails** (moved from ch. 7, Oct 30, 2026; decided Nov 3, 2026). Beats: the soldiers gamble their loot at cards; Ramon Benites, drunk after a game, abuses a Paraguayan woman and beats a Black comrade who gets in his way nearly to death; Benedito steps in; a white officer orders him off, and he obeys; nobody punishes Benites. He nurses the comrade afterwards. What it leaves: in battle the field is level, in the camp it is not (ch. 5), and now he has failed to stop a white man. Mirror of ch. 9, where he stops Damião, a Black man, and is accused anyway; the war's rules show in both directions. (The cards: one more table where other men's luck decides, ch. 18.)
      - **6. Waiting** (decided Nov 3, 2026). Months in the occupied city: the diplomats arrive (Paranhos, 20 Feb) and talk of a Paraguayan government; the cards, the drink, the women, the sick; the *Quixote* read slowly; the hope wears thin a little more each week.
      - **7. Hope taken** (decided Nov 3, 2026). The Count d'Eu arrives (c. 14 Apr) and takes command at Luque (16 Apr 1869); the hunt for López in the hills begins (the Campaign of the Hills, from July). He is sent back to the line, the book in his pack. The war he believed was over takes him to Piribebuy and Acosta Ñu (ch. 9).
      - **Camp news** (decided Oct 30, 2026; dates checked Nov 3, 2026), through the wounded he treats: the Dezembrada, Itororó (6 Dec), Avaí (11 Dec), Lomas Valentinas (21–27 Dec) and Angostura's surrender (30 Dec 1868); López escapes into the hills.
  - Facts:
      
      - [DOCUMENTED] Brazilian troops occupied Asunción from 1 Jan 1869; the city had been evacuated by all its inhabitants two days before. Looting began as soon as the troops entered: palaces, the National Club, the ministries, the Palace of Congress and the López family's houses; furniture, paintings, carpets, mirrors, pianos, silver and gold tableware were shipped to Buenos Aires and Rio de Janeiro. Asunción stayed under Brazilian occupation until 1876. (Sources 1, 2)
      - [DOCUMENTED] Caxias entered Asunción on 5 Jan 1869; ill and weary of the war, he fainted at mass on 17 Jan, gave up command on 18 Jan and left for Montevideo on 19 Jan. Paranhos arrived on 20 Feb 1869. (Sources 1, 2)
      - [DOCUMENTED] Caxias declared the war over in an order of the day, Jan 1869 (exact date to verify). (Source 2)
      - [DOCUMENTED] Avaí, 11 Dec 1868: about 3,000 Paraguayans killed and 600 wounded; Brazilian losses 297 killed, 1,366 wounded. Itororó (6 Dec), Lomas Valentinas (21–27 Dec) and the surrender of Angostura (30 Dec 1868) close the Dezembrada. (Sources 2, 3)
      - [DOCUMENTED] The Count d'Eu took command of the allied army at Luque on 16 Apr 1869, two days after arriving in Asunción; the Campaign of the Hills began in July 1869. (Sources 2, 4)
      - [PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION] Wounded Paraguayan boys from Avaí treated in the Brazilian hospitals in Asunción; residentas searching the hospitals for their sons; soldiers gambling their loot at cards. (Source 2)
