### 7\. Humaitá

  - When / where: 1867–68, age 21–22 · siege of Humaitá
  - Readiness: 83/100 · beats 5 · arc 5 · links 4 · facts 4 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **Core event 4: the assault on Humaitá (16 July 1868): Roque Júnior repays the debt** (decided Oct 30, 2026; expanded into four beats, Nov 2, 2026). He goes back to the line for the allied assault on the fortress: Osório's division, sent by Caxias against about 3,000 defenders, is repelled with more than a thousand Brazilian casualties against fewer than a hundred Paraguayan dead \[DOCUMENTED; see research/paraguayan-war/pantano-tuiuti-humaita.md\] (next four items).
      - **4.1 The assault.** The charge against the fortress's trenches and walls: mud, ditches, fire from above, and the order to keep going while the line breaks around him.
      - **4.2 Mbareté.** Sargento Mbareté, whom he has faced before (Tuiuti, ch. 5), his mirror on the other side: another man fighting for a country that does not value him. A real duel, close and personal, ending with the blow to his head (decided Nov 1, 2026); Mbareté is about to kill him. **He gives up** (decided Nov 2, 2026): his whole body shuts down, and he can feel it happening. He falls and thinks: "Well, this is it." He accepts that luck was not on his side. He thinks of his friends back in Bahia, and of his mother, Luzia. He is ashamed: he put himself there; he was so proud, and so stupid, to think he would survive.
      - **4.3 After the blow.** But he is saved: Roque Júnior, recovered and back in the fighting, saves his life and carries him out: "Now we're even." He wakes up later, and understands that luck has not completely abandoned him yet (decided Nov 2, 2026). The care in the infirmary is paid back. Then days in the infirmary, confused, losing time; the first headache; the healer becomes the patient. It plants the headaches and the decline of ch. 15 and 17.
      - **4.4 The surrender** (5 Aug 1868). The defenders evacuate the fortress by canoe on 24 July 1868; about 1,000 reach Paraguayan ground, and about 1,300 are taken prisoner on 5 August, starving, many of them boys \[DOCUMENTED; the boys: STRONGLY SUPPORTED\]. He gives one of them water. A first glimpse of Acosta Ñu (the boys: ch. 8, ch. 9).
      - **Camp news** (decided Oct 30, 2026), heard in the infirmary: the army's march around the Paraguayan flank to Tuiu-Cuê (Jul–Aug 1867); the ironclads force their way past Humaitá (19 Feb 1868), and for the first time the war seems winnable; at dawn on 2 Mar 1868 Paraguayan canoes hidden under branches carry men with machetes and swords onto the ironclads *Cabral* and *Lima Barros*; Captain Albuquerque steals the Black soldiers' pay; Caxias's order that soldiers be treated more strictly (or the rumour of it) gives the men something to argue about at night; Constantino, back from captivity, tells the Paraguayan side.
      - Option: the second battle of Tuiuti (3 Nov 1867): the Paraguayans raid the old allied camp, where the hospitals and the merchants' stores are (the attackers looting and getting drunk on the stolen liquor: to verify); he lives it as a chaotic night in the infirmary.
