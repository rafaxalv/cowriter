### 13\. The Purchase

  - When / where: c. 1877–78 · Corumbá
  - Readiness: 82/100 · beats 5 · arc 5 · links 5 · facts 3 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 4, 2026)
      - **How the chapter runs.** Benedito reads Luzia's letters and loses his mother a second time; Bastião's news turns grief into a race against the returning steamer. The rescue succeeds, but Benedito's choice to marry Carmen passes the chain on rather than breaking it.
  - Stories:
      
      - **c. 1877. The letters** (decided Oct 30, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026). He opens them with Bastião beside him. Bastião hands him Tonico's letter first, as Tonico told him to (decided Nov 2, 2026). **Tonico's letter, given in full in the book** (decided Nov 2, 2026), short: he breaks the news that Luzia is dead, so her son does not learn it from her own hand, and tells him how she lived and died: free, paid, a nurse in Cachoeira, near enough for Tonico to help her when she needed it; she lies in a marked grave, buried by the Boa Morte sisterhood, a place he can visit. Then he apologises for never helping him in the past (with Luzia's sale, with Ezequiel, all the times he did nothing), explains that he was a boy and too afraid of his father, and that he is still ashamed of it; he would like to have come himself, but he cannot leave the estate, its debts and its business are his now. He signs it as the milk brother, and asks nothing: he knows he was never a coffee brother, and the joke from ch. 3 closes here (decided Nov 2, 2026). **Then Luzia's bundle** (decided Nov 2, 2026): twenty or thirty letters tied with string, the first written in Vassouras with Tonico's help, the rest alone in Cachoeira, some only a few lines on scraps. He did not know they existed. He reads her words himself (Dom Obá taught him). **In the book: a few key passages, woven into the scene, and her last letter in full**, the peak of the chapter (decided Nov 2, 2026). He expected a mother proud of his luck; he finds a woman who kept her faith but stopped waiting on luck and made her own destiny (*Deus ajuda quem cedo madruga*; see Interlude). A shock. Joy and grief arrive together, in his oldest friend's hands. He finally stops searching the lists for her name. Its first effect: the Purchase, below. (Voice of her letters: CHAR-LUZIA-LETTERS, to create from BASE-LETTERS-DESPERATE, adjusted to her literacy: the Vassouras letter cleaner, with Tonico's help; the Cachoeira ones in her own hand, misspelled, getting better.)
      - Lucília teaches her Portuguese in the months before. After the win Albino forbids her to speak with Carmen; when Alvarado arrives, Lucília risks her place and tells Carmen about the lottery, Benedito's wealth and Alvarado's purpose (Part 3, chosen Nov 4, 2026).
      - **The click and the clock** (Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). After the letters and the news of Luzia's death, Benedito tells Bastião about the woman at Albino's. Bastião remembers the drunk Spaniard on their steamer boasting that he will collect the "virgin from Vigo" for Madame Angélique. When Benedito hears the name Alvarado, he recognizes it from her story (ch. 11). The same boat that brought Bastião is carrying the man who will take her away; it will return from Cuiabá in about two to three weeks. Benedito stops waiting on luck and decides to act.
      - **The money that does not arrive** (Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). Benedito presses the lottery agent, who gives excuses about mail, Rio and papers; Albino is close to the agent, but the agent never admits who held the claim back. Albino's prices rise from two contos to "five, in cash, on the table," meant to humiliate because he knows Benedito cannot pay. The agent offers to buy the claim at a ruinous discount; nobody will lend to a Black man, even one rich on paper. Firmino offers all his savings, but it is not enough. Seu Bento, who carries the agent's mail, discovers the claim letter was never sent. Benedito suspects Albino arranged the delay after the Day 1 threat (ch. 12), but has no proof.
      - **Benedito's last offer** (Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). Before Carmen is moved, he offers three contos on paper against the prize and silence: he will marry her as Albino's niece, and nobody need learn where she came from. During the negotiation Benedito challenges Albino: did he have the agent hold the claim? Albino neither admits nor denies it. Benedito knows the two men are close, but has no proof. Albino refuses the paper and the prospect of a Black husband living in town as proof of his crime; he would rather send her away with Alvarado.
      - **Her move** (Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). Carmen recognizes Alvarado and lies that Benedito already had her, destroying her virgin's price. It backfires: Albino accepts the brothel price rather than let a Black man have her, and plans to hide her on the downriver steamer. Lucília overhears and warns Benedito of the night and path to the port; after the rescue, she tells him what Carmen said, so the reader learns it with him. Whether he believes the lie remains open; sexual violence stays offstage.
      - **The night by the river** (Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). On the appointed night Benedito and Bastião wait on the path to the port. Albino's and Alvarado's men come with knives; the two friends fight with capoeira learned in Kalunga's roda. They take Carmen and capture Alvarado alive. Bastião is cut, and Benedito sews the wound with the hands Luzia taught him to use. One of the other men goes into the dark river; whether he lives and whose man he was remain open. Alvarado is known to the local police as a trafficker. No one calls the police that night: each side has something to hide. Bastião's scar may matter later (ch. 14 or 17; open).
      - **The deal** (Part 1 and Part 3 chosen Nov 4, 2026). The next morning Carmen is hidden at Seu Bento's. In law she is free, but Albino can accuse Benedito of abducting his "niece"; Benedito knows the claim letter was never sent, can invoke the crime of selling a free person (Criminal Code of 1830, art. 179; source to add), and holds Alvarado, a trafficker known to local police. Alvarado can testify that Albino arranged Carmen's sale and sent him to take her away, giving Benedito proof of Albino's involvement. Benedito threatens to turn Alvarado over to the police and expose Albino. They make a quiet deal: two contos when the prize arrives, one for Carmen and one for silence; Albino signs as her uncle and guardian consenting to the marriage, and the agent finally sends the claim. Alvarado is paid for his silence and released to leave downriver, telling Benedito, "Ask her about Buenos Aires." Benedito could let Carmen go with money and passage, but chooses marriage and the protection of Albino's paper. He tells Bastião sincerely that his sons will not be stopped at every door; he wants a white wife and lighter children, to spare them what he endured. Bastião hears what is underneath it. Benedito never asks Carmen. Whether guardian consent was legally required at her age remains to verify.
      - **The handover** (revised Nov 4, 2026). Lucília has told Carmen the truth, so she knows Benedito won and understands why Alvarado came; she is not surprised that a rich man has bought her. She still asks, "So what am I going to be? A maid, a helper, or what?" He says, "You will be my wife." She is afraid to challenge him and says nothing. She knows the alternative was the brothel downriver.
      - **Marriage without a choice** (revised Nov 4, 2026). Carmen lives in his house before the wedding; the town knows only that the lucky Black man took Albino's niece home. When the prize arrives (c. early 1878), Benedito pays the two contos and they marry. She is spared the brothel and bought at once; she calls him "senhor." He gives her no choice, and without seeing it makes her his dependent. Debt and gratitude tie them together; she is free in law, not in fact. They never reveal the trafficking story (pillar 1, what is withheld).
  - Notes:
