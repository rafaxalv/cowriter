### Interlude — Tonico's Journey (c. 1866–1877)

  - Readiness: 71/100 · beats 4 · arc 4 · links 4 · facts 3 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - Tonico watches his milk brother leave for the war; he stays.
      - The old master dies (c. 1867–68). Tonico, about 21, takes over the estate and its debts. Going through the accounts (early 1868), he finds how well his father paid Ezequiel, and fires him at once: the legitimate son throws out the unrecognised one, his own half-brother. \[Catharsis point.\] Tonico hands him a dismissal letter that smells like urine. The joke outlives his title: it is still what people say about him (decided Oct 30, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026).
      - **c. 1868. Ezequiel burns the hideout** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Without his title, hated everywhere in the region and furious above all at the letter, Ezequiel goes after the one place he never found: Velho Kalunga's hideout. No member of the clan would give it away; but the man whose hand he whipped off in his first whipping (ch. 3) is a special victim, still traumatised by him. Ezequiel catches him and tortures him until he shows the way. He sets the hideout on fire and kills many of the clan, Velho Kalunga among them. While the others try to put out the fire, Bastião runs after Ezequiel and kills him. The clan breaks up; Bastião lives on alone in the woods, with less and less to keep him in the Recôncavo.
      - **Tonico's regret** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Tonico knows that his letter sent Ezequiel to the hideout. He understands he should have killed Ezequiel, and regrets trying to give him a peaceful way out. He carries it for the rest of his life, and it is part of why he finally helps Benedito. His one failure that can never be repaired.
      - **Tonico looks for her** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Out of guilt over the sale, he goes looking for Luzia on purpose: his sugar business takes him south to Rio, and he follows the sale records into the coffee country. There he finds Luzia in Vassouras, in the Paraíba Valley: older, already free, living as a healer, still the one everyone calls for the sick, still singing. Through his eyes we see the coffee world: hillsides of coffee bushes in rows, gangs of 20–25 enslaved workers driven under an overseer's eye, each tending thousands of bushes, a valley that produced about half the world's coffee and is already starting to wear out its soil.
      - Luzia writes a letter to her son herself, with a little help from Tonico, who is impressed: the woman who never kept a coin now counts every one. In it she tells her son that faith alone was not enough: luck comes and goes, and you have to fight for what you want (*Deus ajuda quem cedo madruga*); she still reminds him: Orí ni baba, Orí ni ìyá (revised Nov 2, 2026; he reads it in ch. 13, just after his win, and the irony is never spelled out) (see research/music/luzia-songs.md). It is the first of many (below). Tonico promises to deliver it. They agree he has a better chance to find him then her - she is old, tired and without many resources.
      - But he hears nothing of her son: letters from the front were rare and casualty lists unreliable. He keeps the letter, and later all of them (until 1877, below).
      - Tonico always knows where Bastião is hiding, and never tells anyone (decided Oct 30, 2026; after 1868 Bastião hides alone; pays off in 1877, below).
      - Luzia's growth: her faith changes after she is separated from her son (decided Nov 1, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026). "How could the orixás allow such an atrocity?" She does not lose her faith: she still sings to the orixás and greets them. But she decides that relying on them is not enough. Luck comes and goes; you have to work hard and fight for what you want. *Deus ajuda quem cedo madruga.* For the first time in her life she saves, with one goal: her freedom, so that she can go back to her son. It takes years; she joins a junta (savings circle). She buys her freedom (the scam is dropped (decided Nov 2, 2026)). She keeps her faith, but stops waiting for luck. It takes too long (decided Nov 1, 2026): she is free only c. 1866, when her son is already at the war (he left in 1865). Even free, she cannot go back to find her son: the journey north costs money she does not have yet, and she is still saving for it (decided Nov 2, 2026). When Tonico finds her (c. 1868), she is free and still short of the fare; she does not know her son went to war, and Tonico tells her the story.
      - From Tonico she learns her son went to the war. **Tonico brings her home** (decided Nov 2, 2026): he pays the journey she was saving for, and what would have taken her years more he makes easy (decided Nov 2, 2026); he takes her back to Bahia, to Cachoeira, a town she knows from her market days, to work as a paid nurse. She is free, paid, among people she knows, and close to the place her son will come back to, if he comes back. Cachoeira is also close to the estate, so Tonico can make sure she is all right and support her when she needs it, which he does from time to time (decided Nov 2, 2026). **She keeps writing** (decided Nov 2, 2026): in Cachoeira she writes to her son again and again, as if he could read it the next day, twenty or thirty letters, some only a few lines on scraps of paper, her hand and spelling slowly getting better. **Her good years, and a good ending** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Luzia is given a few more years: c. 1868–73. In Cachoeira she is free, paid and respected, the nurse everyone sends for; she delivers babies, sings at the festas, and joins the Boa Morte sisters at their August festa. The war ends in 1870; her son's name is on no list of the dead that Tonico can find, and she decides he is alive (Orí ni baba, Orí ni ìyá). She dies c. 1873, old and tired but at peace, in her own bed, a free woman, with the sisters around her singing; her last letter is finished a few days before. A good death, the one the sisterhood of Our Lady of the Good Death prays for. Her son never returns to Bahia (he goes from the war to Corumbá, ch. 10), and she never knows it. A Black brotherhood of Cachoeira buries her in a marked grave, a place her son can visit. Before she dies she puts the whole bundle of letters in Tonico's hands (decided Nov 2, 2026) (the Irmandade da Boa Morte, the sisterhood of Black women of Cachoeira, Rosária's sisterhood (decided Nov 2, 2026); its founding date is debated, so its presence c. 1873 is \[PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION\]).
      - **c. 1877. Tonico, Bastião and the letter** (decided Oct 30, 2026). In Bahia, c. late August 1877, Tonico reads the newspaper (Bastião cannot read) (decided Nov 1, 2026). He has always known where Bastião hides, and has never told anyone; now, for the first time, he goes there. The moment is tense: the master's son, alone, at a runaway's hiding place; Bastião meets him with a knife in his hand, until he sees Tonico is unarmed and came with no one (revised Nov 2, 2026). Then Tonico says why he has come, reads him the paper and tells him Benedito's story. He asks if Bastião would bring Luzia's letters to Benedito, and Bastião promptly offers himself: with Kalunga dead and the clan gone, nothing keeps him in the Recôncavo; he would rather be somewhere he is not so easily recognised, and have a new life close to a friend (decided Nov 1, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026). Bastião is famous in the region, and a forged pass would not save him from the capitães do mato of the Recôncavo; so Tonico takes him down the river as if he had captured him, a runaway in his custody: Tonico tells him a steamer leaves that same day, and they manage to catch it, the Paraguaçu steamer from Cachoeira to Salvador (a line running since 1819; to verify for the 1870s) (decided Nov 1, 2026), until they reach a place where no one knows Bastião's face. There Tonico leaves him with a pass naming Tonico as his owner, sending him on business, and gives him Luzia's bundle of letters, tied with string, kept for four years, and his own, written on the way down the river, with one instruction: give him mine first (decided Nov 2, 2026). Tonico's letter tells him his mother is dead, so he does not learn it from her own hand; and some money to get faster transport (decided Nov 1, 2026). Tonico's first adventure. From there Bastião goes on alone: a runaway crossing half of Brazil with letters he cannot read, a huge adventure, but smart, a very good fighter and taught by Kalunga, he knows his way around, and he gets there. With Tonico's pass he can travel part of the way by steamer (the coast to Rio, then south and up the rivers), so the journey takes about two or three months: he arrives c. late November 1877 (decided Nov 1, 2026).
      - Structure (decided Oct 30, 2026): told here, between Bastião putting the letters in his hands (end of ch. 12) and his reading them (start of ch. 13), so the reader learns Tonico's side, Ezequiel's firing, the burning of the hideout and Kalunga's death, and Luzia's death at the moment Benedito does.
