### 6\. Cholera

  - When / where: 1866–67, age 20–21 · the allied camps, the infirmary
  - Readiness: 73/100 · beats 3 · arc 5 · links 4 · facts 3 · decisions 5 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **Roque Júnior in the infirmary** (decided Oct 30, 2026). Roque Júnior, gravely wounded at Curupaiti (ch. 5), is in the infirmary, and Benedito treats him with what Luzia taught him. He recovers too fast; Benedito, impressed by his strength, says he must be "a rare specimen" (*um espécime raro*). Roque Júnior laughs without humour: it is a word for animals. Then he tells his story. His mother says his father is Pata Seca, a tall enslaved man with thin shins on a fazenda in the interior of São Paulo, chosen for his body and brought to the women so the master would have more slaves; it was never confirmed, but she named her son after him. He has brothers and sisters he has never met, and he looks for them in the camps, among the tall paulistas with thin shins. A father of too many children to be a father to any (see Concept: family and descent). In 1866 he says "my father has many children", not the two hundred of the later legend. \[Pata Seca: FAMILY TRADITION / legend of a real man; Roque Júnior: FICTIONAL; see research/people/pata-seca.md.\]
      - Writer's notes (not for the text) (decided Oct 30, 2026): the body valued like merchandise (Luzia's teeth, ch. 1; his own teeth at enlistment, ch. 4; Pata Seca's height and shins); a father of too many to be a father to any, against the three fathers of ch. 1; Roque Júnior searching the camps for brothers as Benedito searches the lists for Luzia. Pata Seca never appears on stage: he is only a story told, and he has living descendants; the weight stays on the son and the women, with no sexual detail.
      - **Core event 3: the cholera camps** (c. Apr–May 1867); Gregório dies still believing in citizenship, and his death ends the protagonist's faith in the nation; Ana Néri. What he learned from Luzia (boiled water, keeping the sick drinking, cleaning wounds) keeps him alive and lets him save others. He works beside Ana Néri, who asks where he learned it. "From my mother." He does not get sick, says its luck on his side. In the camps he does what he did for his father (ch. 2): he repeats Luzia's instructions aloud, step by step, and sings her songs while he works, "Atotô" among them. He teaches it to his comrades, and they sing together over the sick. Many survive and are grateful to him; he becomes respected, one of the healers. Being a healer helps him survive: from now on most of the war reaches him as news in the infirmary, and he goes back to the line only a few times (Humaitá; the entry into Asunción, ch. 8; Piribebuy and Acosta Ñu). In the quiet hours of nursing he keeps learning to read with Dom Obá's old newspapers (begun in ch. 4), reading them aloud to the sick (decided Oct 27, 2026).
      - **The trauma: the dead he could not save** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Luzia's methods save many, but never enough. Every morning the carts take the bodies away, and he counts them without meaning to. The sheer number of the dead he could not save is what the cholera leaves in him; it is also where he earns the trust of both sides (ch. 5).
      - **He regrets joining the war** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Here his bravery fades for good. He realizes that luck is not surviving: luck would have been not being there at all. Never in his slave years, with the whippings and the torture, had he seen so much blood and suffering. Soldiers tell him they were lucky to survive one battle, and die in the next; luck is an unreliable ally in these lands. He still prays and hopes for the best, but his hopes grow smaller and smaller. (Pillar 2, luck: the faith he took to war starts to crack; it breaks at Humaitá, ch. 7.)
      - Padre Felício, the army chaplain, baptises the dying and buries the dead in mass graves. He keeps the only written list with the protagonist's real name (lost at the end of the war, ch. 9).
      - Damião deserts and is flogged; he refused to help him. The whip Damião used at Curuzu comes back to him.
      - **The balloons (June–July 1867)** (decided Oct 30, 2026; kept, Nov 2, 2026). Caxias wants to see the Paraguayan lines around Humaitá, hidden in jungle and swamp. A tethered balloon rises over the Tuiuti camp, about 120–130 m up, run by two American balloonists, James and Ezra Allen, veterans of the US Civil War, with Major Chodasiewicz (a Polish engineer serving with the Argentines) as observer. Benedito is among the men holding the ropes: men just through the cholera watch something rise into the sky that nobody in the Recôncavo would believe. (The Paraguayans' smoke screens of burning grass: to verify.)
      - **Camp news** (decided Oct 30, 2026): after Curupaiti the zouave companies are broken up and their men spread into line battalions (Oct 1866, Dados gerais); Caxias takes command (late 1866) and reorganises the army: discipline, supplies, hospitals.
