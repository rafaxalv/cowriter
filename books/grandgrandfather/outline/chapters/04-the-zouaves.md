### 4\. The Zouaves

  - When / where: 1865–66, age 19–20 · Salvador, ship São Francisco, the allied camps in the south
  - Readiness: 54/100 · beats 2 · arc 2 · links 3 · facts 4 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - Enlisting; the recruiter checks his teeth (echo of chapter 1). The army roll registers him under a name the reader never sees; his comrades call him Zuavo. The zouave uniform starts his vanity about clothes: for the first time he wears something that marks him as a man, not as property.
      - Quirino, the zouave uniform, the farewell, Rebouças on board. Quirino, the old Black veteran of Independence who founded the first zouave company, is a father figure for a few months. (The option of telling Part II through his letters to Luzia is dropped (decided Nov 2, 2026).)
      - The voyage south: a boy who had never left the Recôncavo sees the sea, Rio and the cities of the River Plate; crowds, bands and blessings on the quays.
      - **The camps in the south, early 1866: Roque Júnior** (decided Oct 30, 2026). Benedito first sees him: an enormous soldier from São Paulo with thin shins, enslaved and sent to the war. Only an impressive stranger: no rumours, no story yet. Benedito learns who he is only in the infirmary (ch. 6). \[FICTIONAL character.\]
      - **The camps, early 1866: Dom Obá teaches him to read** with old newspapers (decided Nov 1, 2026): Cândido da Fonseca Galvão (later Dom Obá II), a zouave who is discharged in March 1866, before Tuiuti; the lessons happen in the camps before he leaves, and Benedito keeps practising alone with the newspapers for the rest of the war. In the war he stops keeping his head down, as Luzia taught him: he begins to stand up against what other soldiers do (Damião, the abuse of Paraguayan women).
      - **Firmino** (decided Nov 2, 2026): another Black soldier freed to serve. He and Benedito have a few short encounters during the war (ch. 4–9); only mentioned, never expanded.
      - **Camp news** (a line or two each, decided Oct 30, 2026): Quirino dies in a Montevideo hospital (Nov 1865); the army crosses the Paraná at Passo da Pátria (Apr 1866).
