## Arc brainstorm (candidates, 6 Oct 2026)

Candidate arcs to choose from. Nothing here is decided until Rafael marks it keep, maybe or drop. Tags: \[plan\] already in the plan; \[new\] new idea; \[pending\] discussed, not yet applied to the plan. Target length: about 300 pages (80,000-85,000 words), 350 as a hard limit.

Group A: Luzia

A1 \[plan\] Enslaved although the 1831 law made her free; nobody on the estate ever says it.

A2 \[plan\] Healer and midwife with Yoruba plants; her extraordinary voice; she teaches her son her songs.

A3 \[plan\] Love with Inácio born from nursing him; the master's nights; her silence.

A4 \[plan\] Keeps what she earns but gives it away easily, trusting luck and the orixás and believing the good she does comes back as luck; after the sale her faith is shaken but not broken: the orixás alone are not enough, luck comes and goes, you have to fight for what you want (revised Nov 2, 2026); she saves for years and buys her freedom.

A5 \[dropped Nov 2, 2026\] Scammed by an official. Replaced: Tonico brings her back to Cachoeira as a paid nurse (Interlude).

A6 \[plan\] Writes her last letter herself, learns her son is at the war, dies.

A7 \[new\] The people she gave to all her life (her junta, old friends) put money in for her: was generosity luck, or her best investment?

A8 \[new\] After the sale she stops singing to the gods; her last song, at her death, is for her son.

A9 \[new\] Who teaches her to write: a free Black teacher, a priest's housekeeper, or the children of the coffee farm she nurses.

Group B: Childhood in Bahia

B1 \[plan\] Friendship with Tonico, then the estrangement after the sale.

B2 \[plan\] Learning the sugar trade beside Inácio, the seed of the farms.

B3 \[plan\] Dona Mariana teaches him letters "as a game", then stops.

B4 \[plan\] Capoeira with Bastião and Velho Kalunga (ginga before kicks; the rabo de arraia lands on Ezequiel); the terreiro and Rosária (friends; love to develop), sold; rivalry with Ezequiel.

B5 \[plan\] Mãe Otília and the Ogum guia.

B6 \[new\] Luzia gives him a secret Yoruba name, the only name he is sure is his; on his deathbed he whispers it, and no one alive can translate it.

B7 \[new\] A childhood bet won against Tonico by luck: his first proof that luck works.

B8 \[plan\] Ezequiel, the master's unrecognised son: lighter skin everyone sees and nobody names; hates Tonico from his birth; three boys, three fathers (white master, Black enslaved father, none); learns at 12–13, from the goiabeira, that the landlord is his father; rewarded and manipulated into informer, then freed and made capitão do mato (c. 1858, ch. 3); his first whipping (ch. 3); beaten by the protagonist in ch. 3; finally fired by Tonico, his own half-brother, then killed by Bastião after burning Kalunga's hideout (Interlude).

Group C: War

C1 \[plan\] The recruiter checks his teeth (echo of chapter 1).

C2 \[plan\] Marcolino climbs his back to plant the flag at Curuzu; history forgets the man underneath.

C3 \[plan\] Accused of what he did not do (Damião: the officer at Curuzu, the burning village).

C4 \[plan\] Dom Obá teaches him to read with old newspapers. In the war he stops keeping his head down, as Luzia taught him: he begins to stand up against what other soldiers do (Damião, the abuse of Paraguayan women).

C5 \[plan\] Cholera; Ana Néri; Luzia's knowledge saves lives ("From my mother").

C6 \[plan\] Acosta Ñu, the boy with the false beard, the memory he never tells.

C7 \[plan\] Letters to Luzia he can never send.

C8 \[new\] Gambling in the trenches (cards, dice): he wins more than he loses and gets a name for luck, setting up the lottery.

C9 \[new\] He hears a woman in a Paraguayan village sing one of Luzia's songs and believes, for a night, that it is her: chance, a song carried by other enslaved Africans.

C10 \[new\] Padre Felício loses the only list with his registered name: the lost-name motif in one scene.

Group D: After the war, Corumbá

D1 \[plan\] Discharged unpaid; he chooses the frontier over Bahia.

D2 \[plan\] Jail without papers; the land plot "under review"; work beside Paraguayans.

D3 \[plan\] Freedom is harder than he dreamed: rent, hunger, no guaranteed work.

D4 \[plan\] Seu Bento sells the ticket and keeps telling him to save: the opposite of Luzia's old way.

D5 \[new\] The ticket number comes from a dream, his old army number, or one of Luzia's songs.

Group E: The lottery and Luzia's letters

E1 \[plan\] The agent claims he cannot read; he reads the numbers aloud.

E2 \[plan\] The newspapers call him "the lucky Black man".

E3 \[plan\] Luzia's letters: the shock of a mother who turned against luck.

E4 \[decided Oct 30, 2026\] Tonico finds Bastião and sends the letter with him; Bastião, who cannot read, carries it to Corumbá (Interlude, ch. 12–13).

E5 \[new\] After reading her letters he burns his own unsent war letters, or keeps them forever.

Group F: The woman from Spain

F1 \[plan\] Galicia: Rosa, Xosé dead in Cuba, Don Faustino and the gancho Alvarado.

F2 \[plan\] Ruchla, sold in Buenos Aires; she searches for her for years.

F3 \[plan\] Doña Engracia teaches her to hide money in her hem.

F4 \[pending\] Kept untouched because a virgin sells for more; spared the brothel, a relief. Proposal: Albino keeps her in the back of his house, working with Lucília, offered quietly to rich men "as a bride".

F5 \[decided Nov 1, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026\] She keeps a secret hem stash all her life, the only one who saves; after the sons lose everything it is a small comfort, not what saves the family, which survives without the fortune.

F6 \[new\] Ruchla reappears years later (a polaca in Rio or Corumbá, or only a name in a newspaper).

F7 \[new\] She hears of a Manuel Lousada in Buenos Aires and never knows if it is her father.

Group G: The Purchase and the marriage

G1 \[pending\] He buys her as a decision after Luzia's letters, not by luck: he could not save his mother, he can save her.

G2 \[plan\] He gives her no choice: in saving her he does to her what was done to him.

G3 \[new\] At the sale Albino invites him to check her teeth "like a horse"; he refuses (echo of chapter 1).

G4 \[plan\] She takes over the money and the farm accounts; luck against ledgers is their recurring argument.

G5 \[new\] She finds Luzia's letters and reads them; she becomes the one who lives Luzia's lesson.

G6 \[plan\] Lucília becomes godmother to their first son.

Group H: Wealth and the farms

H1 \[plan\] Dr. Teles: clean deeds that still call him preto.

H2 \[plan\] The Pires neighbours: polite in public, hostile in private; later lend to the sons.

H3 \[plan\] Did the farms use enslaved labour before 1888? The hardest choice in the book.

H4 \[plan\] He hosts fugitives from nearby ranches for a while, then, paranoid and jealous, sends them all away in anger. (Bastião joins him at Liberdade c. 1878, ch. 14.)

H5 \[decided\] Ezequiel's arc ends in Bahia: Tonico fires him; he burns Kalunga's hideout and kills Kalunga; Bastião kills him (Interlude; revised Nov 2, 2026).

H6 \[dropped Nov 2, 2026\] Luiz Gama's challenge: you bought a woman to free her, but did you give her a choice?

H7 \[dropped Nov 2, 2026\] Dom Obá in Rio admires and mocks his wealth.

H8 \[dropped Nov 2, 2026\] Visit to Luzia's grave in Vassouras.

Group I: Family and the fall

I1 \[plan\] The São João beating: the charcoal beard brings back Acosta Ñu.

I2 \[plan\] Wife and children reject his African culture.

I3 \[plan\] His death; he tells his wife his true name.

I4 \[plan\] Bad at the land, the sons gamble to cover losses; white "friends" (the Pires sons and Otávio, their childhood friends) with marked cards slowly take everything, out of the sons' pride and wish to be like the white elite (cards and the Pires credit).

I5 \[plan\] The son he beat loses everything the first night he wears a beard.

I6 \[new\] A son inherits Luzia's letters and loses them in a card game, or sells it.

I7 \[dropped: the fortune is lost at cards\] Jogo do bicho: a dream points to an animal, and a son bets the farm on it.

Group J: The frame

J1 \[plan\] Grandma, Alzheimer's, the fog; memory never valued.

J2 \[plan\] Epilogue: grandma says the story in one sentence.

J3 \[new\] The author searches old newspaper archives for "the lucky Black man" and finds a lottery result with no name (one author-voice scene).

J4 \[new\] Grandma played the lottery all her life, as a ritual of hope, linking her to her grandfather.

J5 \[new\] The family's sayings ("moça de família") return as echoes in the chapters.

Group K: Objects that run through the book

K1 Luzia's letters. K2 The Ogum guia. K3 The zouave uniform. K4 The lottery ticket. K5 The hem money. K6 The charcoal beard (São João) and the false beards (Acosta Ñu).
