### 12\. The Lottery

  - When / where: c. 1877 · Corumbá
  - Readiness: 65/100 · beats 3 · arc 3 · links 4 · facts 4 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 4, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **The win (Day 0, c. mid-July 1877)** (decided Nov 1, 2026). The draw is in Rio, c. June 1877; the results come upriver about 3–4 weeks later, printed in the Rio papers on the steamer (Corumbá has no telegraph; travel time to verify). The win: 20 contos, the top prize of the Court lottery (Loteria da Corte), which from 1867 to 1880 had one draw of 6,000 tickets with a top prize of 20 contos (Loner 2014) (decided Nov 2, 2026); what it means in years of a labourer's wages. Enough for land, cattle and a comfortable life. The win soon becomes a burden: everyone knows. People ask for loans and favours, try to cheat him, strangers claim to be cousins and old comrades. He loses his privacy and cannot tell friends from opportunists. His distrust and paranoia start here.
      - Collecting the prize: the ticket came from the Court lottery in Rio, sold in Corumbá through a Portuguese merchant who is the lottery agent (Seu Bento resells his tickets in the street, in fractions). The results come upriver weeks later, printed in Rio newspapers on the steamer. The agent pays small prizes from his till; a big one he must claim through his correspondent in Rio, for a commission, and it takes months. So the winner depends on one man. The agent looks at the ticket, then at him, and tells him he has misread it: the numbers don't match, and a preto like him can't read anyway. Perhaps he hints the ticket was stolen, or offers a small "consolation" for it. But he can read: Dom Obá taught him in the trenches with old newspapers. He reads the numbers aloud, slowly, off the printed list, in front of everyone. Seu Bento, who sold him the ticket and wrote it in his book, comes to vouch for him. Reading, the skill the war gave him, is what saves his fortune. Echo of the deeds Dr. Teles will write later: money doesn't stop the insults, it only changes their form.
      - How the town looks at "the lucky Black man". The agent scene was public: by nightfall the port and the taverns know, and Albino among them (decided Nov 1, 2026).
      - The talk around town treats a white wife and lighter children as things a new fortune might buy: society voices the promise of whitening before Benedito admits it as his own motive (Part 1, moved into Chapters Nov 4, 2026).
      - **Day 1: Benedito's empty threat** (decided Nov 4, 2026). The next morning, before the prize money arrives, he offers Albino one conto on a note against the claim, the price the woman herself told him. Albino refuses to sell her to a Black man or accept paper. He has employed Benedito many times as a porter and knows him as the poor Black man who carries his barrels. He cannot accept that this same man is now rich enough to buy a white woman: to Albino, the offer is an intolerable reversal of rank, as well as a threat to his pride and respectability. Rich for one day and sure luck is finally on his side, Benedito loses his temper in Albino's tavern and threatens to tell the town that the "niece" is trafficked and Albino is selling her. He has no proof, and exposing her would ruin her too. From that day Albino hides her and keeps Lucília away. Unseen for now: in his rage, Albino writes to Madame Angélique offering her back for quick cash. The claim letter is not sent; Benedito later suspects Albino asked his friend, the lottery agent, to hold it, but neither man confirms this. The reader has the same suspicion, not proof (Part 3, revised Nov 4, 2026).
      - The win makes the newspapers and the news travels (decided Nov 1, 2026). Within a week or two Corumbá's own first newspaper, *O Iniciador* (founded 1877), prints his name and a description (a Black ex-zouave from Bahia); the reader never sees the name, only that it is printed there. The steamer carries it to Rio in about a month, where the papers reprint "the lucky Black ex-zouave", and a week later the Bahia papers copy it (c. late August 1877). That is how Tonico learns where he is (Interlude), and Luzia's letters start their journey.
      - **c. late November 1877, about four or five months after the win. Bastião arrives in Corumbá with the letters** (decided Oct 30, 2026; date (decided Nov 1, 2026)). The reunion first: the only man who knew him as a boy, with a story of the journey, told in his storyteller's way, not in detail, and the news that Tonico sent him. Bastião brings word that Velho Kalunga is dead, killed by Ezequiel, and that Ezequiel is dead too, by Bastião's own hand; the full story is the Interlude (decided Nov 2, 2026). Bastião also brings word of Rosária, sold to Salvador in 1865 (decided Nov 1, 2026): she is married, with children, a life of her own in Salvador. Then Bastião puts the letters in his hands: Tonico's first, as Tonico told him, then Luzia's bundle (decided Nov 2, 2026). Chapter ends (the Interlude follows; the letters are read in ch. 13).
      - The lottery: the Court lottery in Rio (decided Nov 2, 2026; its prize size fits, Loner 2014). How a frontier winner was paid (the agent and his correspondent in Rio, commissions, months of delay) remains a plausible reconstruction; Loner documents treasurers, procuradores, commissions and delays elsewhere.
      - **Timeline, from the win to the wedding** (revised Nov 4, 2026): the draw in Rio (c. June 1877) → Day 0, he learns he won (c. mid-July 1877) → that evening the town knows, Albino too → Day 1, Benedito's first offer and empty threat; Albino hides her, writes to Angélique and the claim letter goes missing; Benedito later suspects Albino asked the agent to hold it → weeks 1–2, *O Iniciador* prints his name → c. late Aug 1877, Rio and Bahia papers; Tonico reads it → Sept 1877, the hideout and the river; Bastião sets off → c. early Nov, Bastião and Alvarado board the same steamer upriver → c. late Nov, they arrive; Bastião gives Benedito the letters (4–5 months of waiting, never seeing her) → ch. 13: the letters, the click, the race, the capture, the night by the river, the deal and handover → the prize money arrives (c. early 1878) → the wedding → Liberdade (1878). Steamers (Reynaldo 2003, Fronteiras 7(14)): the Montevideo–Corumbá–Cáceres–Cuiabá line from 1870 (Conceição & Cia, Decree 453 of 7 June 1870); the Companhia Nacional de Navegação a Vapor from 1873, running Montevideo–Rio with a subsidy per round trip; Corumbá–Cuiabá took 7–12 days. Travel time Corumbá–Montevideo–Rio and sailing frequency: still to verify. Owners raising prices for lottery winners: Loner 2014.
