### 5\. Curuzu

  - When / where: 1866, age 20 · Tuiuti, Curuzu, Curupaiti
  - Readiness: 72/100 · beats 4 · arc 4 · links 3 · facts 4 · decisions 4 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)
  - Stories:
      
      - **Core event 1: Tuiuti (24 May 1866), his first battle** (expanded (decided Nov 2, 2026)). General Osório speaks to the men before the battle. Sargento Mbareté faces him for the first time; the Paraguayans call the Black troops macacos (as in the Cabichuí caricatures). \[Catharsis point: the zouaves hold in the mud with bayonets and capoeira until the line turns; the first time he fights back and it works. "Ògún yè!" under his breath. Cost: the same week, in camp, the field is not level.\]
      - **The swamp as the enemy** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Paint it well: the battle and the long months after it are fought in the marshes of the Estero Bellaco and around Tuiuti. Mud to the knees, water nobody can drink, heat, mosquitoes in clouds at night, snakes and caimans in the reeds, leeches, and the fevers and dysentery that kill more men than the bullets. The swamp has no side. Checked: disease killed far more than combat (about 70% of allied dead; Osório: "A peste é a maior inimiga que temos"); the camp water was "amarelenta e grossa, poluída pela vizinhança dos cadáveres" (Cerqueira); vultures and caracaras cleaned up the offal \[DOCUMENTED; the animals: PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION, still to find in Cerqueira or Taunay; see research/paraguayan-war/pantano-tuiuti-humaita.md\].
      - **After the battle: the burning of the dead** (decided Nov 2, 2026). So many dead (about 6,000 Paraguayans, numbers disputed) that the Paraguayan bodies are heaped in alternate layers with wood, in piles of 50 to 100, and burned; the men complain that the Paraguayans are so lean they will not burn (Thompson, 1869) \[DOCUMENTED\]. The smell hangs over the camp for days \[PLAUSIBLE RECONSTRUCTION\].
      - **Luzia's lessons start to matter here** (decided Nov 2, 2026), before the cholera: boiled water, keeping the feverish drinking, clean wounds. He does it without thinking, and some of the men near him notice.
      - **Research to do: the swamp's tales** (decided Nov 2, 2026). Look for extraordinary events or soldiers' tales from those swamps, the legends men told at night (giant snakes, lights over the water, the dead who walk, animals no one had seen): something remarkable to add a little spice, labelled by truth level. First findings (research/paraguayan-war/pantano-tuiuti-humaita.md): no soldiers' swamp legends found yet; candidates are the boitatá, the fire-snake the Bahians would name the marsh lights over the dead (real folklore, plausible episode), Guaraní legends heard from prisoners or camp women (the Pombero who whistles at night, the Mbói Tu'i guarding the marshes), and the true strange things: the dead too lean to burn, the camalotes, floating islands of plants on the river.
      - Marcolino climbs his back to plant the flag at Curuzu (Sept 1866). History remembers Marcolino, not the man underneath. \[Catharsis point: the wall taken, the Paraguayan flag torn down; the cost is that history forgets him.\] After Curuzu, Damião captures a light-skinned Paraguayan officer, ties him to a tree and whips him the way a master whips a slave, while a few Black comrades watch. The protagonist was not there: he arrives too late, and the man is already dead. He is accused with the rest, simply because he belongs to the Black troop; Damião's friends stay silent. The same week, white officers have Black soldiers flogged for small faults. In battle the field is level; in the camp it is not.
      - **His position: in between** (core event 2, expanded (decided Nov 2, 2026)). A complex feeling: the formerly enslaved are not always good (Damião), and the white officers are not good either. He belongs to neither team: not the Black soldiers', who find him soft because he will not join their revenge, and not the white officers', who see one more Black soldier to blame. Later, as a nurse, he earns both sides' trust, but he is still not one of them: they keep fighting with him, and he keeps fighting with them. (A quiet echo of the festa, ch. 3, where he threw "not black enough to fight, not white enough to be a master" at Ezequiel; never pointed out.)
      - **Curupaiti (22 Sept 1866), from the stretchers** (decided Oct 30, 2026; part of core event 2). The allied disaster: thousands fall in a few hours against fortified trenches. He is not in the charge: he is with the stretchers, carrying in the wounded, the first time his healing is needed in the war. Roque Júnior, the giant he saw in the camps, is carried in gravely wounded. Chapter ends; the nursing continues in ch. 6.
      - **Camp news** (decided Oct 30, 2026): the months in the Tuiuti trenches, swamp, mud, heat, bad water and the camp followers, told in a few lines (war is mostly waiting); the ironclad *Rio de Janeiro* sunk by two floating mines the day after bombarding Curuzu (2 Sept 1866), 53 men lost \[DOCUMENTED\]; the peace talk between López and Mitre at Yataity Corá (12 Sept 1866) and the brief hope that the war will end.
