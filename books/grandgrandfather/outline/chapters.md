# Chapters

Oct 2, 2026 · Rafael

Working list of chapters and the stories planned for each. Working names, for planning only and never in the book text: Benedito (him) and Carmen (the woman from Spain). Built from the Character Bible arc. Style: immersive fiction, history blended into the scenes (no factual step-outs). Every chapter ends on a hook. Neither he nor the woman from Spain is ever named.

Structure note: Part IV (her story) is one chapter, "Her Road" (ch. 11): over many meetings at Albino's (c. February–July 1877) she tells him her story, part by part, told from her side. It comes right after their first meeting (ch. 10) and before the lottery, so her background is known before "The Purchase" (decided Nov 1–2, 2026).

Open option: a short prologue and epilogue in the grandmother's voice, telling the story to a grandchild. Open option for Part II: tell much of the war through his letters to his mother (see chapter 4). Catharsis points (Oct 10, 2026; Tarantino-style release, rationed, see NARR-MAIN; to revisit): ch. 3 (he beats Ezequiel), Interlude (Tonico banishes Ezequiel), ch. 5 (Tuiuti; Curuzu), ch. 9 (he stops Damião), ch. 13 (the night by the river; revised Nov 4, 2026).

## PART II — WAR (1865–1870)

How the war is told (decided Oct 30, 2026; revised Nov 2, 2026): he is not on every front. Only the events he actually lives are told as scenes, and those are told in detail and with precision (dates, places, units, weather, wounds: researched, not guessed), above all the ones that leave the traumas the book explores later. Everything else reaches him as news in the camps and the infirmary, a line or two each. The letters to Luzia as the spine of Part II are dropped (decided Nov 2, 2026). **The five core events** (decided Nov 2, 2026): (1) Tuiuti and the swamp, May 1866 (ch. 5); (2) Curuzu and Curupaiti, Sept 1866 (ch. 5); (3) the cholera camps, 1867 (ch. 6); (4) the assault on Humaitá, July 1868, the blow to the head (ch. 7); (5) Piribebuy and Acosta Ñu, Aug 1869, the deepest wound (ch. 9). Asunción (ch. 8) stays short: its trauma is smaller. **His arc in the war: the bravery fades** (decided Nov 2, 2026): he bet on the war as his door to freedom and goes proud and brave; slowly he starts to regret it. The bravery wears away battle by battle, above all in the cholera (ch. 6), until he gives up completely when he is hit in the head at Humaitá (ch. 7), and wakes up to find that luck has not quite abandoned him yet. **Dates and time** (decided Nov 3, 2026): dates matter only for the major events (the five core events, the entry into Asunción); everything else is told without dates. In the war he often loses track of time: days run together in the swamp, the infirmary and the waiting, and he can rarely say what month it is. The researched dates stay in Facts for accuracy, not in his head or on the page.
