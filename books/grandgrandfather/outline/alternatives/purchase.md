# Alternative: Albino's Offer (The Purchase)

Working alternative for the Purchase arc. **Not decided**: Chapters remains the plan of record. This version replaces the previous Part 1, Part 2 and Part 3 alternatives with one new path.

## Core

After the lottery win becomes public, Albino offers Carmen to Benedito. The change in Albino's treatment reveals that money has moved Benedito across a social threshold: the man who once carried Albino's barrels is now someone Albino invites to his table and treats as a possible buyer. Benedito sees both the opportunity and the bargain's racial logic. He accepts without telling the reader, and the chapter ends when he enters Carmen's room.

## Proposed sequence

1. **The earlier conversations.** For a few months before the win, Benedito sees Carmen at Albino's place while doing occasional work for him. They talk, but never for long. In the first staged exchange she is wary of the poor, ill-dressed Black laborer in the room. In a second, they find a little common ground. Albino stays close, watches them and always finds a reason to send Benedito away. Three or four more interrupted attempts are summarized, not staged as full scenes.
2. **Albino's offer.** After the lottery news spreads, Albino invites Benedito in, serves food and lets him speak with Carmen. He asks whether Benedito likes her, then offers her for two contos. Her usual asking price was one conto; Albino raises it because this buyer is newly rich. He tells Benedito he may do as he wishes with her, including marry her, and urges him to consider a white wife and lighter descendants who may be treated better. Benedito recognizes that his new fortune has given him access to offers and rooms that were closed to him before. The exchange feels like a reversal: he is now on the other side of Albino's table.
3. **The door.** Benedito accepts Albino's offer on a promise against the prize, but the reader is not told. Albino sends him to speak with Carmen. Still in his old clothes and unaware of the win, she sees the familiar Black laborer in her room without Albino nearby. She is confused and afraid: how did he get in, and where is Albino? End the chapter with Benedito in the doorway and Carmen looking up at him.
4. **Carmen's story and the reveal.** The next chapter follows Carmen. Benedito asks too many questions as she tells him her story. At the end he says she belongs to him now: he intends her to be his wife and the mother of his children, living with him on a farm. She does not believe him; she does not know he won. Albino returns with a cynical question. Possible line: "Well? Is it to be a wedding, or should I keep looking?" Carmen believes the arrangement only then. She says little and barely remembers what she said.
5. **The arrangements.** Albino takes over the practicalities: Carmen must pack, he will prepare the papers, and Benedito must find her a home rather than leave her on the street. He offers to help Benedito secure a farm and persuade its owner to let them move in before the prize money arrives. The precise papers, move and occupancy terms remain open.

## Changes and dependencies

- The offer chapter (currently ch. 13) ends at Carmen's doorway; the following chapter carries her story and the delayed reveal. The chapter numbers and final placement of her story are provisional.
- The current Chapter 11 sequence, where Carmen tells her story before the lottery, would be rebuilt or moved into the chapter after the offer.
- Bastião arrives with Luzia's letters after Carmen's story in this alternative. His exact arrival date, his reunion with Benedito and how the letters affect the next decision still need to be planned.
- The purchase remains a choice Benedito makes after luck gives him power. Albino's offer makes the whitening motive direct; Benedito also feels the sudden access and deference his money gives him.
- This is an alternative only. It does not change official Chapters, the Character Bible or the Concept.

## Open decisions

- How many months pass between the earlier conversations and the win, and where in Albino's place do they talk?
- Does Albino make the offer on Day 0, after the town learns of the win, or on Day 1?
- What paper secures the two-contos promise against the prize, and when is it paid?
- What kind of farm or property can Albino arrange for Benedito to occupy before the money arrives?
- What is Albino's final cynical line when he returns to find Carmen unconvinced?
- When exactly does Bastião arrive after Carmen's story, and how do Luzia's letters shape Benedito's next choice?
