# Timeline — Paraguayan War (1811–1892)

Oct 2, 2026 · Rafael

The Paraguayan War ran from November 1864 to 1 March 1870, but its causes reach back to Paraguay's isolation after 1811 and the struggle over the River Plate. This is the single master timeline for the war; every other research doc feeds into it. Built following the "How to build a timeline" instructions in this folder.

## How to use and update this timeline

  - **One row per event**, in date order inside each period. The Reliability column uses the same scale as the research notes: *Documented* (archives or scholarship), *Contested* (historians disagree), *Tradition* (oral or folk, usable as what a character says), *To verify* (from general knowledge, not yet checked).
  - **The Source column names the project doc** a fact came from, so when a doc changes you know which rows to revisit: [Research Notes (ranked)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/175wfkIyU5xPVZNTRUYKfU_TNRn0QhcmzLXe-NHRXE04/edit), [Pesquisa completa (Paraguayan view)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1QuYfgCsGy3e2b9RXu6M8F25Erm2LdSmJqicbiuDdCUo/edit), [Dados gerais](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1tRYX-bhSmNrz3jVWB8tvFh-QnjJvkwLepsPQNSXMRVM/edit), [Character Bible](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1RtZ-XY2fM_n6TynDPww-q2u9l2Cqm4r0W-FCX2LIAcc/edit), [Book Concept](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1zH0rnWHUvEbAYKUpYd-tIfSkpWe-aHZUf02xeA_V_jM/edit), [Região](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1yIEwaavy49fkEHfy9hEvpYEf4BIdhixyL6C3Q5GU9eE/edit). "Web" means a page listed under Sources at the end.
  - **To add something:** add a row in the right period, or leave a comment "add to timeline" and ask Claude to slot it in. When a row is checked, change its Reliability word.
  - The **Story hooks** section maps each period to the book's invented characters; it changes whenever the Character Bible does.

## The war at a glance

Paraguay attacked for nine months; the Allies needed four years to end the war. Everything after April 1866 was fought on Paraguayan soil.

| **Phase** | **Dates** | **Turning points** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Brazil intervenes in Uruguay | Oct 1864 – Feb 1865 | *Marquês de Olinda* seized, 12 Nov 1864 |
| Paraguayan offensives (Mato Grosso, Corrientes, Rio Grande do Sul) | Dec 1864 – Sep 1865 | Riachuelo, 11 Jun 1865; Uruguaiana, 18 Sep 1865 |
| Allied invasion and siege of Humaitá | Apr 1866 – Jul 1868 | Tuyutí, 24 May 1866; Curupayty, 22 Sep 1866 |
| Dezembrada and fall of Asunción | Dec 1868 – Jan 1869 | Lomas Valentinas, 21–27 Dec 1868 |
| Cordillera campaign | Apr 1869 – Mar 1870 | Acosta Ñu, 16 Aug 1869; Cerro Corá, 1 Mar 1870 |

## Background, 1811–1862

Two things set the stage: a small, closed Paraguay that needed the rivers to reach the sea, and a slave-holding Brazilian Empire whose Black veterans of Independence still remembered fighting for the nation.

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| May 1811 | Paraguay declares independence from Spain; Buenos Aires refuses to recognise it for four decades. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 1814–1840 | Dr. José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia rules Paraguay as dictator and seals it off: state estancias, self-sufficiency, almost no foreign trade. | Documented | Pesquisa completa |
| 1822–1823 | War of Independence in Bahia. Quirino, Rocha Galvão, Buri and Carvalhal fight the Portuguese; the leather-clad sertão cowboys inspire the later "couraças". | Documented | Dados gerais |
| 1831 | The last segregated Black and pardo militia battalions are abolished when the National Guard is created. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| Jan 1835 | Malê Revolt of enslaved Muslim Africans in Salvador; harsh repression of Africans follows. | To verify | Character Bible |
| 1837 | The Army stops excluding "homens pretos" and becomes formally colour-blind; service records no longer note a soldier's colour. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| 1837–1838 | Sabinada revolt in Salvador. The father of cadet Constantino Bigode, last commander of the Black militia battalion, is killed in its aftermath. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |
| 1844–1862 | Carlos Antonio López modernises Paraguay: Ybycuí iron foundry, railway, telegraph, the Humaitá river fortress, a large army, high literacy. | Documented | Pesquisa completa |
| 1844 | Brazil becomes the first country to recognise Paraguay's independence, as a counterweight to Buenos Aires. | To verify | General knowledge |
| Sep 1850 | Eusébio de Queirós Law ends the legal Atlantic slave trade to Brazil; the internal trade from the north-east to the coffee south grows. The Land Law follows the same month. | To verify | Character Bible |
| Feb 1852 | Rosas falls at Caseros; Argentina finally recognises Paraguay. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 1855–1858 | River-navigation crisis: Brazil sends a squadron up the Paraguay River; a treaty later opens the river to Brazilian ships bound for Mato Grosso, its only practical route there. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 1861–1862 | Bartolomé Mitre wins at Pavón and unifies Argentina; he becomes president in 1862. | To verify | General knowledge |
| Sep–Oct 1862 | Carlos Antonio López dies; his son Francisco Solano López takes power with ambitions for Paraguay's role in the region. | To verify | General knowledge |

## The road to war, 1863–1864

A civil war in Uruguay pulled in Brazil, and López treated Brazil's intervention as a threat to the balance of the River Plate. That López struck first is not disputed; whether it was defence or a reckless gamble is (see Pesquisa completa, section 2).

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 19 Apr 1863 | Venancio Flores (Colorado) invades Uruguay against the Blanco government, with Argentine sympathy and help from Brazilian ranchers on the border. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 6 Sep 1863 | López protests Argentine involvement in Uruguay; Blanco Uruguay looks to Paraguay as its ally. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| Apr 1864 | Brazilian envoy José Antônio Saraiva arrives to demand compensation for Brazilians harmed in Uruguay. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 4 Aug 1864 | Brazil issues an ultimatum to Uruguay. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 30 Aug 1864 | Paraguay warns Brazil that occupying Uruguayan territory would break the regional balance. | Documented | Web; Pesquisa completa |
| 12 Oct 1864 | Brazilian troops under João Propício Mena Barreto cross into Uruguay. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 12 Nov 1864 | The Paraguayan gunboat *Tacuarí* seizes the Brazilian steamer *Marquês de Olinda*, carrying the new governor of Mato Grosso. Paraguay breaks relations with Brazil. | Documented | Web; Research Notes |
| Dec 1864–Jan 1865 | Siege of Paysandú by Brazilian and Colorado forces; the town falls on 2 January. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 14 Dec 1864 | Paraguay invades Mato Grosso by river and land. Fort Coimbra is attacked on 27 December. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 29 Dec 1864 | Dourados colony: Lt. Antônio João Ribeiro and a handful of men die resisting; his famous last words are tradition. | Tradition | Web; tradition |
| Jan 1865 | Paraguayans occupy Albuquerque and Corumbá; settlers flee. Relevant if the family ends up near Corumbá (Região doc). | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 7 Jan 1865 | Decree creating the Voluntários da Pátria. In practice much "volunteering" was forced recruitment of the poor and Black. | To verify | Dados gerais (COPIR); Research Notes |
| 26 Jan–1 Feb 1865 | Quirino Antônio do Espírito Santo offers to raise a corps of "cidadãos crioulos" and sets up at Forte do Barbalho, Salvador: the start of the Zuavos baianos. | Documented | Dados gerais (Kraay) |
| 20 Feb 1865 | Flores takes Montevideo; Uruguay changes sides and will join the alliance against Paraguay. | To verify | General knowledge |
| Late 1864–1865 | Thesis that Britain engineered the war (Pomer, Chiavenato). Largely rejected by specialists; usable in a character's mouth. | Contested | Research Notes; Pesquisa completa |

## 1865: Paraguay attacks, the alliance forms

López's offensives created the very alliance he feared. By the end of 1865 his navy was gone, his southern army had surrendered at Uruguaiana, and the war moved onto Paraguayan soil.

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 23 Mar 1865 | Paraguay's Congress declares war on Argentina after Mitre refuses passage across Misiones. | Documented | Web; Pesquisa completa |
| 13 Apr 1865 | Paraguayan ships and troops seize the port of Corrientes; Argentine opinion turns to war. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 1 May 1865 | Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay sign the Treaty of the Triple Alliance. Its secret clauses on Paraguay's borders, once published, become central to Paraguayan memory. | Documented | Web; Pesquisa completa |
| May 1865 | Two Zuavo companies sail south on the *São Francisco* (ex-*Cotopaxi*), reportedly with engineer André Rebouças; Pedro II attends their farewell in Rio. | To verify | Dados gerais (Bento) |
| 11 Jun 1865 | Battle of Riachuelo: Admiral Barroso's squadron destroys the Paraguayan navy. | Documented | Web; Research Notes |
| Jun 1865 | A Paraguayan column under Estigarribia invades Rio Grande do Sul at São Borja. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| Jul 1865 | Pedro II leaves Rio for the southern front; the Conde d'Eu travels with him and writes *Viagem Militar ao Rio Grande do Sul*, praising the zuavos as "a mais linda tropa". | To verify | Dados gerais; general knowledge |
| Aug 1865 | Ana Néri, a Bahian widow, volunteers as a nurse and follows her sons to the front. | To verify | Character Bible |
| 6 Aug 1865 | Paraguayans occupy Uruguaiana. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 17 Aug 1865 | Battle of Yatay: Flores destroys the Paraguayan column on the west bank of the Uruguay River. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 18 Sep 1865 | The Uruguaiana garrison surrenders before Pedro II, Mitre and Flores. The first zuavo companies are part of the siege lines. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais (Kraay) |
| Oct–Nov 1865 | Paraguayan forces withdraw from Corrientes back across the Paraná. | To verify | General knowledge |
| Nov 1865 | Quirino, founder of the zuavos, dies in a Montevideo hospital. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| Before Mar 1866 | Bahia has shipped eleven zuavo companies (638 men) and a couraça company (80 men); Bahia mobilises 10,189 men by March 1866. | Documented | Dados gerais; Research Notes (Kraay) |
| 1865–1866 | Many "volunteers" are not: most Brazilians who served did so unwillingly. Slaves use the zuavos to escape owners; one sent to buy banana leaves comes back in uniform. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |

## 1866–1868: invasion, Curupayty and the siege of Humaitá

The Allies crossed into Paraguay in April 1866 and then spent two years in trenches and swamps in front of the Humaitá fortress. Disease killed more men than battle; this is when Brazil began recruiting enslaved men systematically.

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 & 12 Jan 1866 | Marcolino is removed as interim zuavo commander; Dom Obá is dismissed by Gen. Porto Alegre for rowdiness and discharged in March for poor health. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |
| 16 Apr 1866 | The Allied armies cross the Paraná at Paso de Patria and invade Paraguay. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais |
| 2 May 1866 | Estero Bellaco: a Paraguayan surprise attack checks the Allied advance. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 24 May 1866 | First Battle of Tuyutí, the bloodiest battle of the war. Zuavo commander Rocha Galvão is killed. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais |
| 18 Jul 1866 | Battles of Sauce and Boquerón. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 3 Sep 1866 | Capture of Curuzu. Bahians and Pernambucans lead the assault; the 66-year-old Captain Maniva is among the first over the trenches. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |
| 3 Sep 1866 | Captain Marcolino climbs the wall on a soldier's back, swaps the flag and shouts "Está aqui o negro zuavo baiano!" The deed was praised in orders of the day; the exact words come from Querino. | Tradition | Dados gerais; Research Notes |
| 12 Sep 1866 | López meets Mitre and Flores at Yataytí Corá; no peace. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 22 Sep 1866 | Curupayty: about 4,200 Allied casualties against under 100 Paraguayan. Cadet Constantino Bigode is captured and spends two years at the Ybycuí foundry. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais |
| 30 Oct 1866 | The zuavo companies are dissolved; their officers are scattered across eight battalions. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |
| 6 Nov 1866 | Decree 3.725-A grants free liberty to the state's slaves ("escravos da Nação") designated for the army, and extends it to their wives. | Documented | Web (Câmara) |
| Late 1866 on | Systematic recruitment of enslaved men: bought, donated or sent as substitutes for National Guardsmen, freed on enlistment. | Documented | Research Notes (Kraay) |
| Nov 1866 | The Duke of Caxias takes command of the Brazilian forces (appointed 10 Oct, in command 19 Nov). | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 1867 | Cholera sweeps the Allied camps; zuavo officers Buri and Manoel Teodoro de Jesus die of it. In March, Lt. Augusto Francisco da Silva is killed by a bomb splinter. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| Apr–Jun 1867 | Mato Grosso: the Retreat of Laguna (told by Taunay); Corumbá is retaken in June, then abandoned amid smallpox. | To verify | Web (pt.Wikipedia); general knowledge |
| Apr–May 1867 | Paraguay launches war papers *El Centinela* and *Cabichuí*, drawing Brazilians and Pedro II as "macacos". | Documented | Pesquisa completa |
| 3 Nov 1867 | Second Battle of Tuyutí (Kraay dates Cupim Júnior's wound there to 24 Sep 1867; check). | Contested | Web; Dados gerais |
| 19 Feb 1868 | Brazilian ironclads force the passage of Humaitá; Asunción is evacuated. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 2 Mar 1868 | Paraguayans in canoes try to board the ironclads *Lima Barros* and *Cabral*. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| Mid-1868 | San Fernando: López has alleged conspirators executed, including his brothers, and his mother and sisters mistreated. Whether a real conspiracy existed is debated (Maestri). | Contested | Pesquisa completa; Research Notes |
| 25 Jul 1868 | Humaitá falls after the long siege. | Documented | Web; Pesquisa completa |

## 1868–1870: the Dezembrada, Asunción and the hunt for López

After Humaitá, Caxias broke López's army in December 1868 and took Asunción. The Conde d'Eu then chased a rebuilt army of boys, old men and women through the Cordillera until López was killed at Cerro Corá.

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 6 Dec 1868 | Itororó: the Dezembrada begins with a costly bridge battle; Caxias leads the charge himself. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 11 Dec 1868 | Avaí: a Paraguayan division is destroyed. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 21–27 Dec 1868 | Lomas Valentinas (Itá Ybaté). Ex-zuavo Capt. José Soares Cupim Júnior is wounded on the first day. López refuses to surrender and escapes. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais |
| 30 Dec 1868 | Angostura surrenders with 1,907 men. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 1 Jan 1869 | Brazilian troops occupy Asunción, and the city is looted. The occupation lasts until 1876. | Documented | Web; Pesquisa completa |
| 13 Jan 1869 | Cupim Júnior dies of his wounds; his widow later gets 720 mil-réis a year. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| 18–19 Jan 1869 | Caxias, ill, resigns and leaves for Brazil. | Documented | Web (pt.Wikipedia) |
| Apr 1869 | The Conde d'Eu, aged 27, takes overall command for the Cordillera campaign. | Documented | Web (Portal São Francisco); Research Notes |
| 1869 | Constantino Bigode is freed after two years as a prisoner; promoted alferes in March 1870. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| From Jul 1869 | Desertion rises; families hide in the woods; Paraguayan women flee toward Brazil and Corrientes so their sons will not be taken (family tradition fits this pattern). | Tradition | Research Notes |
| 12 Aug 1869 | Piribebuy, López's temporary capital, is stormed. About 700 Paraguayan dead to just over 300 wounded suggests executions; whether d'Eu ordered beheadings and the hospital fire is unresolved. | Contested | Research Notes (Maestri) |
| 15 Aug 1869 | A provisional Paraguayan government is installed in Asunción under Allied protection. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 16 Aug 1869 | Acosta Ñu (Campo Grande): mostly boys, some in false beards, face Allied veterans. Numbers vary widely; today it is Paraguay's Children's Day. | Contested | Pesquisa completa; Character Bible (Clodfelter) |
| 1869–1870 | Looting and rape of Paraguayan women are treated as nearly normal in Imperial memoirs; no record found of the specific family story about Black soldiers. | Documented | Research Notes; Pesquisa completa |
| 1 Mar 1870 | Cerro Corá / Aquidabã: López is killed by Brazilian troops, ex-zuavo Capt. Barbosa among them. The war ends; the day is now Paraguay's Heroes' Day. | Documented | Web; Dados gerais |
| 1870 | Three Bahian volunteer battalions come home to parades, are dissolved, and many men are never paid what they are owed. Few zuavos are among them. | Documented | Dados gerais (Kraay) |

## Aftermath, 1870–1892

The war left Paraguay ruined and occupied, and it left Brazil with a politicised army and a slavery system that the war had weakened. Abolition came 18 years after Cerro Corá, the Republic a year later.

| **Date** | **Event** | **Reliability** | **Source** |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1870 | Paraguayan census: 221,079 people left, of whom 106,254 women, 86,079 children and 28,746 men. Estimates of the total death toll range from under 10% to about 69% of the pre-war population. | Contested | Pesquisa completa; Research Notes |
| 1870 | Republican Manifesto in Rio; the Republican Party is founded. | To verify | General knowledge |
| 28 Sep 1871 | Lei do Ventre Livre: children born to enslaved mothers are free (with conditions). Spain's Moret Law had done the same for Cuba in 1870. | To verify | Book Concept; Character Bible |
| 1871 | Parliament grants pensions to the widows of five zuavo officers, including Quirino's widow, Sabina Joana do Espírito Santo. | Documented | Dados gerais |
| 9 Jan 1872 | Loizaga–Cotegipe Treaty fixes the Brazil–Paraguay border. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 1872 | Dom Obá obtains the honours of alferes; in the 1870s he moves to Rio and becomes a famous Black monarchist columnist. | Documented | Research Notes; Dados gerais |
| 3 Feb 1876 | Machain–Irigoyen Treaty settles the Argentina–Paraguay border; the last Allied occupation troops leave Paraguay in 1876. | Documented | Web (Wikipedia) |
| 1880 | André Rebouças, a war veteran, joins Joaquim Nabuco in the abolitionist movement. | To verify | Dados gerais |
| 1885 | Sexagenarian Law frees enslaved people over 60. | To verify | Book Concept |
| 1887 | Paraguay's Colorado and Liberal parties are founded; the war becomes the root of its politics. | To verify | Pesquisa completa |
| 1888 | Captain Marcolino dies in Salvador, having ended up as a street sweeper and library doorman, per a later chronicler. | Tradition | Research Notes |
| 13 May 1888 | Lei Áurea abolishes slavery in Brazil. | To verify | Book Concept |
| 15 Nov 1889 – Jul 1890 | Army officers, many of them Paraguay veterans, proclaim the Republic. Dom Obá loses his rank after a monarchist demonstration in December and dies in July 1890. | Documented | Research Notes; Dados gerais |
| 1892 | The *jogo do bicho* lottery is invented in Rio by the Barão de Drummond. | To verify | Character Bible |
