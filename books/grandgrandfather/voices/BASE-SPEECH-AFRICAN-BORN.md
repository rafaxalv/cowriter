## Who and when

People born in Africa and brought to Brazil, enslaved or freed: Mãe Otília, the market women and *ganhadeiras* of Salvador, the junta women, the Malê cloth seller, old Africans on the estate.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - A second language: Portuguese learned as captives, fluent after years but carrying the rhythm, order and words of their first language (Yoruba, Hausa, Kimbundu and others).
  - Authority of their own: priestesses, healers, market women, elders; respected in their world whatever the whites think.
  - Proverbs and the sacred: orixás, ancestors, Allah for the Malê Muslims.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - A proverb instead of an opinion.
      - First-language words for what matters most.
      - Speaks with authority in their own space.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Meu filho*, *minha filha* to the young; titles of the terreiro (*mãe*, *iyá*); *sinhô* to whites, as little as possible.
  - **Never says**
      - Their name from home to a white person.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Their first language; prayer; song.

## Typical mistakes

  - Word order from the first language (verb late, repetition for emphasis).
  - Gender and agreement simplified.
  - A first-language word where the Portuguese one is missing or not good enough.
  - Recent arrivals: short phrases, few verbs, a lot carried by gesture (fades with years).

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Fluent or learning Portuguese with the first language's rhythm, carried in syntax and word choice, never phonetic spelling; sacred words kept.
  - Sample (original): "Orixá não esquece. Gente esquece."

## Language layer: EN

  - The same rhythm in English: short, paired, repeated clauses; sacred words kept, in italics; no dialect spelling.
  - Sample (original): "The orixá does not forget. People forget."

## Variants

  - **Priestess** (Mãe Otília): ritual authority, fewer words, each one heavy.
  - **Market women**: quick, bargaining, teasing.
  - **Malê Muslims**: Arabic prayer words, Hausa; reserved with outsiders; see CHAR-LUZIA-SPEECH for a fluent Yoruba woman.
