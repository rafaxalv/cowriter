## Who and when

Portuguese-born men of trade in Brazil: Seu Albino, the tavern and store keepers, clerks and traffickers of Corumbá.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - European Portuguese among Brazilians: different words, forms and pronunciation; they look down on Brazilian speech and are mocked for theirs.
  - Commerce and credit; a good host, a hard bargainer.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - European Portuguese forms (*tu* and the second person, *rapariga*, *estou a fazer*), different from the Brazilians around them.
      - Prices and debts.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Ó rapaz*, *ó menina*; *senhor* to customers.
  - **Never says**
      - The trade he hides.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Threats (the brothel, for Carmen); haggling.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - European Portuguese forms in syntax and words (*estou a ver*, *tu*, *rapariga*), not phonetic spelling.
  - Sample (original): "Ó rapariga, estás a ver aquela porta? É a do bordel."

## Language layer: EN

  - A slightly different, old-fashioned English to mark him as foreign among Brazilians (word choice, *tu* rendered as a familiar tone); no dialect spelling.
  - Sample (original): "See that door, girl? That one's the brothel."

## Variants

  - **Seu Albino**: crude, cruel, a good host; passes Carmen off as his niece; haggles over her.
