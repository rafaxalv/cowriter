## Who and when

People of Corumbá and the Pantanal frontier, 1870s–1890s: Seu Bento, Dr. Aurélio Teles, Lucília, Otávio, the Pires sons, merchants, the lottery agent, peões and boatmen; and the younger sons Inácio, Manuel and José as they grow up.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - A mixed port town: Brazilians from many provinces, Paraguayans, Bolivians, Portuguese, Italians; trade words from the river.
  - Peões and boatmen: Pantanal cattle words, Guaraní and Spanish from the border.
  - The town's elite copies Rio and Buenos Aires; manners and French for their children.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - River and cattle words (*comitiva*, *boiada*, *vapor*, *a cheia*).
      - Spanish and Guaraní words from the border.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Seu*, *dona*, *doutor*; *senhor* to the rich; *compadre*, *comadre*.
  - **Never says**
      - (By character; see variants.)
  - **Under pressure**
      - (By character.)

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Frontier Portuguese with border words; the notary formal and exact; the peões plain; no phonetic spelling.
  - Sample (original): "O vapor chega na cheia, e a cheia chega quando quer."

## Language layer: EN

  - Plain English with the border and river words kept; the notary formal.
  - Sample (original): "The steamer comes with the flood, and the flood comes when it likes."

## Variants

  - **Seu Bento**: elderly free Black ticket seller; his most honest friend; wise, plain, a few sayings; tells him to save.
  - **Dr. Aurélio Teles**: lawyer and notary; precise, formal, honest: "Write everything down."
  - **Lucília**: free Black laundress; warm, plain, quick; teaches Carmen Portuguese with simple words and patience.
  - **Otávio and the Pires sons**: elite boys; posh, mocking, *moleque* when they think no one hears.
  - **Inácio** (the follower): see CHAR-INACIO-SON-SPEECH. **Manuel** (the dreamer): bookish, believes the quotes, long sentences. **José** (the youngest, his father's favourite): closer to his father's plain speech than his brothers.
