  - **Type**: Base speech

  - **Built with**: character-voice skill, as a generic speech voice for characters without their own (Rafael, 2026-11-03)

## Who and when

Galician and Spanish speakers in Carmen's road (ch. 11), speaking their own language: Rosa Lousada, Xosé Lousada, Don Faustino, Doña Engracia, Señor Alvarado, Capitán Iriarte, the people of the village and of Buenos Aires.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Galician at home and in the village; Castilian Spanish from the priest, the state and the recruiter.
  - The sea, fishing and lace; emigration, Cuba and the *quintas*; Rosalía de Castro's "widows of the living".
  - In their own language they are fluent; on the page their speech is rendered fluently in the book's languages, with Galician and Spanish words kept.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - The sea and the saints; Galician words kept (*morriña*, *Virxe santa*).
      - The recruiter's charm in Castilian; the village's Galician.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Miña nena*, *fillo*, *don*, *doña*, *señor*.
  - **Never says**
      - (The recruiter) what the job is.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Prayers to the Virgin; the sea's omens.

## Language layer: PT-BR

## Variants

  - **Rosa Lousada**: lacemaker; counts thread and coins; lets her go and regrets it.
  - **Don Faustino**: the village priest; Church Castilian and Latin; kind, and the door to the recruiter.
  - **Señor Alvarado**: the *gancho*; charming, warm, Castilian, letters "from girls who married well".
  - **Capitán Iriarte**: bribed, once kind; a captain's few words.
  - **Doña Engracia**: older, emigrating legally; practical, motherly.
