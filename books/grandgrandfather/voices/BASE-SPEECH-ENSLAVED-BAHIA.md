## Who and when

Brazil-born enslaved and freed people of the Recôncavo, 1840s–1860s: Inácio (his father), Rosária, the estate's workers and house servants, the boys of the roda, the capoeiristas of Kalunga's clan, the free Black people at the festas in Cachoeira.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Portuguese is their own language: the popular speech of the Recôncavo, shaped by the African languages of their parents and grandparents.
  - Two registers: careful, short and deferent with whites (*sinhô*, *sinhá*); warm, quick, joking and singing among themselves.
  - Faith mixes Catholic saints and the orixás; songs at work and at the roda carry what cannot be said.
  - Never shown as less intelligent for having no schooling: wit, irony and double meanings are their defence.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - The double register: few careful words to whites, a different speech the moment they leave.
      - Sayings and song lines in place of complaints.
      - Irony the masters do not catch.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Sinhô*, *sinhá*, *nhonhô* to the masters; *mãe*, *pai*, *tia*, *tio* to elders; *mestre* to a capoeira master; nicknames among the young.
  - **Never says**
      - A complaint to a white face; where a runaway hides.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Silence or song; a saint or an orixá named.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Popular Recôncavo speech in syntax and word choice (*a gente*, popular agreement, short coordinated clauses), never phonetic spelling; African words in songs and prayers.
  - Sample (original): "Sinhô mandou, a gente faz."

## Language layer: EN

  - Plain, short English, with the double register kept (careful to whites, quick among themselves); *sinhô*, *sinhá* kept; no dialect spelling.
  - Sample (original): "Sinhô says so, so we do it."

## Variants

  - **House servants** (Rosária): closer to the masters' forms, more careful, more correct.
  - **Field and boiling-house** (Inácio): fewer words, work words.
  - **The roda** (the boys): Kalunga's jokes and capoeira songs; see CHAR-BASTIAO-SPEECH and CHAR-KALUNGA-SPEECH.
