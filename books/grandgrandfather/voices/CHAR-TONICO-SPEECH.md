## Who and when

How Antônio (Tonico) de Menezes talks whenever he speaks (ch. 2, 3, Interlude), and the voice under his letter (ch. 13): the letter is his speech on paper, plainer and more honest than he ever spoke. Character Bible: "Antônio (Tonico) de Menezes".

## Portrait

  - **The master's son.** Educated at home (a tutor, his mother's prayers and letters), a planter's correct Portuguese; can turn to Latin tags from the church and the tutor. *(Bible; the tutor is inference)*
  - **Weak and charming.** Easy, warm, jokes and nicknames; many words circling a decision he never makes. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **Stiff with his father**, formal and short; afraid of him. *(Bible)*
  - **Older (Interlude)**: running an estate in debt, firing Ezequiel, finding Luzia, going alone to Bastião; quieter, guilt under the charm. *(Bible)*
  - **The letter (ch. 13)**: plain, honest, the apology he could never say aloud. *(Chapters)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a nickname or a joke, and a long sentence that ends without deciding.

## Author's choices

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Nicknames and jokes: "milk brother" (ch. 2).
      - Long, pleasant sentences that end without a decision.
      - With his father: short, formal, afraid.
  - **Figures of speech**: games, horses, the river, saints' days. Samples (original): "We'll ride it out, like the river in March." "Let's not wake the old horse." "Saint Anthony will find it, he always does."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "milk brother" (ch. 2, and as the signature of his letter, ch. 13); "Does this make us the coffee brothers?" is Bastião's line, which Tonico is never part of.
  - **Forms of address**: to Benedito, by name or "milk brother"; to his father, *senhor meu pai*; to his mother, *mamãe*; to the enslaved, by first name; to Ezequiel, coldly by name; to Luzia (Interlude), *Luzia*, then with care.
  - **Manners and tics**: laughs first to soften what he says; changes the subject when it gets serious; promises.
  - **Never says**: no to his father; that Ezequiel is his brother; "I'm sorry" aloud (only in the letter).
  - **Under pressure**: more words, more jokes, then silence; with his father, "Yes, senhor".

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Correct planter's Portuguese of the period: *vosmecê* to equals in private, *Vossa Mercê* in form; Latin tags (*Deo gratias*, *in nomine Patris*) rationed.
  - Samples (original): "Ora, irmão de leite, deixa disso." · "Havemos de ver, havemos de ver; não há pressa nenhuma." · "Sim, senhor meu pai."

## Language layer: EN

  - Easy, correct English with period politeness; long sentences that trail off; no Portuguese except *senhor* and the Latin tags.
  - Samples (original): "Come on, milk brother, let it go." · "We'll see, we'll see; there's no hurry at all." · "Yes, senhor."

## Neighbour voices

  - **Benedito**: the lords' words Benedito knows come from Tonico; Tonico says more and decides less.
  - **Ezequiel**: Ezequiel copies the speech Tonico was born to; Tonico's is easy, Ezequiel's effortful.
  - **Coronel Menezes**: the father's speech is Tonico's, hardened and pious; Tonico has its forms without its weight.
