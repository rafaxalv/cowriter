## Who and when

How Afonso, the heir, talks whenever he speaks (ch. 16–18), and in his thoughts at the last card game (ch. 18, told from inside him). Character Bible: "Afonso".

## Portrait

  - **Raised by his mother and Lucília**, born c. 1879; wants the white elite's acceptance. *(Bible)*
  - **Polished and anxious.** His mother's posh Portuguese and quotes picked up from the Pires boys; careful correctness that hides the farm and the senzala. *(Rafael)*
  - **Afraid of his father** since the São João night: short and afraid with him. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **The last game (ch. 18)**: his father dead, his beard grown, sure of winning: boastful and sure of himself at the table. *(Chapters, Rafael)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: correct, borrowed words, and a glance at how they landed.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.
  - **Language**: his mother's polished Portuguese, the Pires boys' quotes and slang of the elite boys; never his father's words.
  - **Figures of speech**: cards, horses, fashion, the Pires boys' world.
  - **Change over time**: follows his life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Correct, borrowed words; a look at whether they landed.
      - Never a word from his father's world (cattle, the roda, Luzia's sayings).
      - With his father, short and afraid; after his death, too loud.
  - **Figures of speech**: cards, luck, horses, clothes, the Pires boys' world. Samples (original): "Tonight the cards owe me." "A gentleman never counts his winnings at the table."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "The cards will open the way" (ch. 18): his father's luck in his own mouth, without knowing it.
  - **Forms of address**: to his father, *senhor*, *meu pai*; to his mother, *mamãe*; to the Pires boys, by surname or nickname, eagerly; to Otávio, by name; to the peões, orders.
  - **Manners and tics**: the beard: he touches and pampers it while he talks (ch. 18), the son who always shaved clean; checks the faces of the white boys before he laughs.
  - **Never says**: why his father beat him (he never knew); anything about the farm's work; a word of Yoruba or a song of his father's.
  - **Under pressure**: louder, more borrowed phrases; at the end of the last game, silence.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Posh, correct Portuguese of the provincial elite youth; French and Latin bits from the Pires boys; no popular forms ever.
  - Samples (original): "Hoje as cartas me devem." · "Sim, senhor." (to his father) · "As cartas vão abrir o caminho."

## Language layer: EN

  - Correct, slightly showy English; borrowed phrases; no dialect, nothing of the farm.
  - Samples (original): "Tonight the cards owe me." · "Yes, senhor." · "The cards will open the way."
