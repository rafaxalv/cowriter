## Who and when

The sugar planters of the Recôncavo and their families, 1840s–1860s: Coronel Joaquim Araújo de Menezes, Dona Mariana, visiting planters, estate chaplains and tutors.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Correct, formal Portuguese of the Brazilian landed class; Catholic piety in every other sentence.
  - Command as courtesy: orders given as requests, cruelty as "order"; they think of themselves as good masters.
  - Debts never mentioned; lineage, saints and the Emperor often.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - Orders as courtesies; God and the saints invoked for everything.
      - "Order", "our people", "my blacks" (period terms, only in their mouth and as the standing rules allow).
      - Never speaks of money or debt.
  - **Forms of address**
      - To the enslaved: first names, *moleque*, *negra*, or no name; to each other: *Vossa Mercê*, *compadre*, titles; to the Emperor and the Church: reverence.
  - **Never says**
      - That a child is his (Ezequiel); that he is in debt.
  - **Under pressure**
      - More formal, more pious; punishment announced calmly.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Correct, formal Portuguese of the period (*Vossa Mercê*, *pois não*, *Deus seja louvado*); Latin tags rationed.
  - Sample (original): "Que se faça a vontade de Deus. E a minha."

## Language layer: EN

  - Formal, old-fashioned English; Latin kept; period slurs only in their mouth, as the standing rules allow.
  - Sample (original): "God's will be done. And mine."

## Variants

  - **The master** (Coronel Menezes): pious, polite, cold; never recognises Ezequiel.
  - **The mistress** (Dona Mariana): gentler words, household piety; teaches prayers and letters "as a game".
  - **Their children as boys**: see CHAR-TONICO-SPEECH.
