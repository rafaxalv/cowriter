## Who and when

How the patriarch of the Pires family talks whenever he speaks (ch. 14–17). Character Bible: "The Pires family".

## Portrait

  - **Old ranching family, Guarda Nacional colonel**, rich in land and slaves, short of cash, living on credit. *(Bible)*
  - **Courteous and commanding.** Polite in public, hostile in private; false concern ("the lands are not good, the heirs will cause problems"). *(Bible, ch. 14)*
  - **Cattle and militia words under the Latin.** He quotes Horace and Seneca at the teas, but his everyday speech is the ranch and the militia. *(Rafael; quotes from ch. 15)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a courtesy that is an order, and a Latin line that does not quite fit the man.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable; two voices for the couple.
  - **Language**: correct, old-family Portuguese; Latin tags; militia and cattle words.
  - **Figures of speech**: cattle, land, rank, lineage; Horace and Seneca (see research/daily-life/citacoes-salao-pires.md).
  - **Change over time**: few changes; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Concern and advice that are really a threat or an offer to buy.
      - "Coronel" in everything: rank and order.
      - A Latin line, slightly misplaced.
  - **Figures of speech**: cattle, land and fences, rank, blood. Samples (original): "Land without an old name is like cattle without a brand." "Money, any lucky man can have." "*Vilius argentum est auro, virtutibus aurum*, as Horace says, my friend."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "my friend" to Benedito, never meant; Horace and Seneca (research/daily-life/citacoes-salao-pires.md); the lottery jab, never to his face.
  - **Forms of address**: to Benedito, *meu amigo* or *senhor*, with a smile; to Carmen, *minha senhora*; to his men and slaves, first names or orders.
  - **Manners and tics**: a hand on the arm while advising; looks at the horizon (the land) while he talks.
  - **Never says**: that he is in debt; what his men do at night; a direct threat.
  - **Under pressure**: more courteous, slower; the militia rank comes forward.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Correct, old-family Portuguese of the frontier elite; Latin in italics; with his men, short rough orders.
  - Samples (original): "Meu amigo, estas terras não são boas. Eu lhe digo por amizade." · "Dinheiro qualquer um com sorte tem." · "Cuidem do gado."

## Language layer: EN

  - Courteous, slightly old-fashioned English; Latin kept; rough and short with his men.
  - Samples (original): "My friend, these lands are not good. I tell you as a friend." · "Money, any lucky man can have."
