## Who and when

How Ezequiel talks whenever he speaks (ch. 2, 3, Interlude). Character Bible: "Ezequiel".

## Portrait

  - **The master's unrecognised son**, pardo, born enslaved c. 1834, freed c. 1858; overseer, then capitão do mato. *(Bible)*
  - **Borrowed lord's speech.** He copies the master's polite, correct Portuguese to set himself above the enslaved; it slips into the senzala's words and curses when he is angry or mocked. *(Rafael)*
  - **Taunts, never jokes.** Enjoys cruelty; taunts Benedito with his freedom; calls people *moleque*. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **Wants his father to see him**, and speaks to the master with a hunger for approval. *(Bible)*
  - **The joke that follows him** (Kalunga's rumour) is the one thing that cracks his polish. *(Bible)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: correct, slightly too careful words with an insult inside, and the slip when he is mocked.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.
  - **Language**: the master's speech, copied with effort; the senzala underneath.
  - **Figures of speech**: the whip, dogs and hunting, order and property, his freedom.
  - **Change over time**: follows his life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Correct words, slightly too careful, an insult inside a polite form.
      - Reminds everyone that he is free.
      - When mocked, the polish breaks into curses and the senzala's words.
  - **Figures of speech**: hunting, dogs, the whip, property and order. Samples (original): "A runaway leaves a smell. I have a good nose." "Order, moleque. Order is what keeps you fed." "Free men don't run."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: *moleque*; "I am a free man"; *ordem*.
  - **Forms of address**: to the enslaved, *moleque*, *negro*, or no name; to the master, *meu senhor*, hungrily; to Tonico, *sinhozinho*, with contempt he cannot quite hide (and later refuses); to Kalunga, *velho*.
  - **Manners and tics**: taps the whip handle while he talks; repeats a word to make it heavier.
  - **Never says**: that the master is his father; Kalunga's joke; please to anyone he rules.
  - **Under pressure**: mocked, he loses the borrowed register at once: curses, the senzala's words; with power, slow and cold.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - The master's correct Portuguese, a little stiff, with forms he has overheard (*pois não*, *Vossa Mercê*) used to the wrong people; when he loses control, the Recôncavo popular speech he was born to. Slurs and *moleque* only as the book's standing rules allow, never in the narration.
  - Samples (original): "Pois não, moleque. Corre. Eu gosto quando correm." · "Eu sou um homem livre." · "Vosmecê me respeita, meu senhor me fez capitão."

## Language layer: EN

  - Careful, correct English, a shade too formal; insults inside polite forms; the break into rough speech when mocked; Portuguese insults and titles kept (*moleque*, *meu senhor*).
  - Samples (original): "By all means, moleque. Run. I like it when they run." · "I am a free man." · "You will respect me. My master made me captain."
