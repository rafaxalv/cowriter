## Who and when

How Inácio, the second son, talks whenever he speaks (ch. 16–18). Not his grandfather Inácio (Benedito's father, BASE-SPEECH-ENSLAVED-BAHIA): scene maps write him as "Inácio (the son)". Character Bible: "The sons", Inácio.

## Portrait

  - **The follower.** Named after his father's father; copies Afonso in everything, and gambles to keep up with him. *(Bible)*
  - **Afonso's echo.** Repeats Afonso's words and opinions a beat later, a little cruder; laughs when Afonso laughs; finishes his sentences. *(Rafael)*
  - **Alone, plainer.** Without Afonso he is unsure, and closer to the farm than he admits: a peões' word slips out. *(Rafael; inference from his father's world)*
  - **Lost at the end.** At the last game, panic; he begs Afonso to stop. *(Rafael)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: Afonso's words, a beat late and a little cruder.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable, as Afonso's echo.
  - **Language**: his mother's and Afonso's posh Portuguese, worn less well; the Pires boys' slang; the farm underneath.
  - **Figures of speech**: borrowed (cards, horses, the Pires boys' jokes).
  - **Change over time**: echo, then lost; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Repeats Afonso a beat later: "That's what I said." "Like Afonso says."
      - The same idea, cruder: Afonso says *pouco elegante*, Inácio says *feio*.
      - Laughs at a joke before he has understood it, because Afonso laughed.
  - **Figures of speech**: borrowed: cards, horses, the Pires boys' jokes. Samples (original): "The cards owe us, Afonso says." "Tonight we win it all back."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "Like Afonso says."; "That's what I said."; his brother's name in almost every speech.
  - **Forms of address**: to Afonso, by name, often; to his father, *senhor*, quietly; to his mother, *mamãe*; to the Pires boys, their surnames, as Afonso does; to the peões, orders only when Afonso is there.
  - **Manners and tics**: looks at Afonso before he answers; copies his gestures (and, in ch. 18, would grow a beard if Afonso did).
  - **Never says**: no to Afonso, until the last game; anything his own.
  - **Under pressure**: the posh words drop away; plain, frightened words, a peões' word; begs.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Afonso's posh Portuguese, worn less well: the end of a sentence copied, a plainer word in the middle; the Pires boys' slang; when scared, the farm's popular speech.
  - Samples (original): "Foi o que eu disse." · "Como o Afonso diz: hoje as cartas devem a gente." · "Para, Afonso. Para, pelo amor de Deus."

## Language layer: EN

  - Afonso's correct English, a beat behind and cruder; no dialect; plain and frightened at the end.
  - Samples (original): "That's what I said." · "Like Afonso says: tonight the cards owe us." · "Stop, Afonso. For God's sake, stop."

## Neighbour voices
