## Who and when

How Velho Kalunga talks whenever he speaks (ch. 2, 3, Interlude). Character Bible: "Velho Kalunga".

## Portrait

  - **An old runaway capoeira master** who lives free in the woods and moves from place to place teaching in secret. *(Bible)*
  - **Joking teacher.** Every line a joke with a lesson inside: spicy, crude, silly or philosophical; never solemn, even about death. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **Legends.** The boys tell legends about him (the kick, the dogs, the African prince, the ghost); he neither confirms nor denies, and laughs. *(Bible)*
  - **Teaches the body first**: the ginga, the rhythm and the songs before the kicks ("Your body can't sing yet"). *(Bible)*
  - **His jokes are weapons**: the rumour about Ezequiel's first whipping is his. *(Bible)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a joke that turns out to be a lesson, and a laugh that ends the conversation.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable, pushed by the role: the most playful voice in the book.
  - **Language**: plain, old Recôncavo speech with African words and capoeira songs.
  - **Figures of speech**: the body, animals, the roda, food, sex, death, all as jokes.
  - **Change over time**: follows his life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - A joke with a lesson inside, said once, never explained.
      - Answers a question with a question or a story.
      - Laughs at the end, and the laugh closes the subject.
  - **Figures of speech**: the body, animals, the roda, food, farts and sex, death. Samples (original): "The strongest kicks are delivered with a smile." (Bible) "A cat doesn't fight the dog; it climbs." "Your body can't sing yet." (Bible)
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "The strongest kicks are delivered with a smile."; "Your body can't sing yet."; the clan's creed (the art protects freedom, never imposes on others), said once, without a joke; songs of the roda.
  - **Forms of address**: the boys by nicknames; Bastião and Benedito as *menino*; whites as *sinhô* only in mockery; Ezequiel as *menino* too, which wounds him.
  - **Manners and tics**: moves while he talks (a ginga even sitting); slaps his own thigh at his joke; sings a line instead of answering.
  - **Never says**: whether the legends are true; his past on the farm he escaped; goodbye.
  - **Under pressure**: jokes, even at the fire (Interlude); the one serious line is the creed.
