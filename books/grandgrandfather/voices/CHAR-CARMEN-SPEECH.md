## Who and when

How the woman from Spain talks in every chapter where she speaks (ch. 10, 11, 13–18), and in her quoted thoughts. Her name (working name Carmen) never appears in the text; the narration calls her the woman from Spain. Character Bible: "Carmen, the woman from Spain".

## Portrait

  - **Galician first, Spanish at school and church, Portuguese last.** Born c. 1858 near Vigo; learns Portuguese in Corumbá from Lucília (1877). *(Bible)*
  - **A learner who becomes posh.** Real learner's mistakes in her first year; by the time she is rich she speaks fluently, with the Pires' quotes and an accent that never quite leaves. *(Rafael)*
  - **Sharp and counting.** Sharp-tongued, stubborn, practical; prices, sums, piecework from her mother's lacemaking; remembers every humiliation. *(Bible)*
  - **Young, and at times naive.** She is about 18–19 when they meet: hopeful, quick to believe a promise (she fell for the gancho's letters), romantic about being a *señora*. The naivety wears off with the years. *(Rafael)*
  - **Catholic and superstitious about the sea**, homesick for rain. *(Bible)*
  - **Under stress she slips back into Galician.** *(Rafael)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a number or a price, a Galician word, and a sharp turn at the end of the sentence.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.
  - **Language**: broken Portuguese at first (Spanish/Galician interference), fluent and posh by ch. 15–16, with an accent that stays; Galician under stress.
  - **Figures of speech**: the sea, her mother's lace and piecework, prices and sums, saints; sharp, counting, and young (sometimes naive).
  - **Change over time**: follows her life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes her recognisable**
      - Counts: prices, sums, days, what something cost and what it is worth.
      - Ends a sentence with a sharp turn, a small sting.
      - A Galician word or a saint when she is moved; full Galician when she breaks.
      - Young: hope and pride in her plans; she believes what she wants to believe (ch. 10–13), and learns not to.
  - **Figures of speech** (the sea, lace, money, saints). Samples (original): "Lace is counted thread by thread, and so is a man." "The sea gives, and keeps the change." "A promise weighs nothing until it is paid."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: *Virxe santa!*, *meu Deus* (later), *mi madre* (her mother); "Men believe in luck; women count." said **once in the whole book** (ch. 11); the Pires' quotes from ch. 15 on (Gracián, Seneca, Buffon), always slightly showy.
  - **Forms of address**: to Albino, *señor* then *seu Albino*; to Lucília by name, warmly; to him, before the marriage, by no name (see the naming rule) or *você*; after it, *senhor* in public; to the Pires, *Coronel*, *Dona*, carefully; to her sons, their names, and corrections.
  - **Manners and tics**: corrects other people's words (from ch. 15, his and the sons' Portuguese); smooths her dress or counts on her fingers while she talks; crosses herself at the sea and at bad news.
  - **Never says**: that she was sold (she says "brought"); the brothel; Ruchla's fate aloud; "I love you" to him while he lives; the truth in her letters home.
  - **Under pressure**: faster, then Galician; numbers become weapons (what she cost, what she is owed). Lying: correct and posh, with a quote.

## Typical mistakes

A Galician and Spanish speaker learning Portuguese (ch. 10–13; fading by ch. 15, the accent stays):

  - **Spanish words for Portuguese ones** (false friends and near words): *ventana* for *janela*, *pero* for *mas*, *ahora* for *agora*. (First to fade.)
  - **Spanish verb endings and tenses**: *hablo* for *falo*; the preterite where Portuguese uses another form.
  - **Wrong genders** where Spanish and Portuguese differ: *el leite*, *a sangue*.
  - **Galician-Spanish pronouns and order**: *me dá* where she means *dá-me*, calques from Galician.
  - **Missing Portuguese nasal sounds and contractions**: *em o* for *no*, *de a* for *da*. (Last to fade, carried in syntax, not spelling.)
  - **Reaching for a word and taking the Galician one**, then going on.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Ch. 10–13: simple Portuguese with the mistakes above, carried in word choice, gender and syntax, never in phonetic spelling. Spanish or Galician words in italics.
  - From ch. 15: fluent, posh, the Pires' quotes in Spanish (Gracián), Latin (Seneca) and French (Buffon), not always well placed; the accent shows in a word or construction now and then.
  - Samples (original): "Quanto custa? Não, quanto *vale*." (ch. 10) · "A renda se conta fio a fio." · "*Nace bárbaro el hombre*, como diz o Coronel." (ch. 16)

## Language layer: EN

  - Ch. 10–13: her learner's English (as the reader hears her Portuguese) through syntax and word choice: simple clauses, a Spanish word where the right one is missing, articles and prepositions a little off; no dialect spelling. Galician and Spanish words kept, in italics.
  - From ch. 15: correct, a little stiff and showy, quotes in their original language.
  - Samples (original): "How much it costs? No. How much it is *worth*." (ch. 10) · "Lace is counted thread by thread." · "*Virxe santa*, the sea again."

## Neighbour voices

  - **Benedito**: fluent, plain, few words; she talks more, sharper, and in later years in quotes. Their arguments: her long polished sentences against his short concrete lines.
  - **Lucília**: her teacher; warm, plain Portuguese; Carmen's early mistakes show beside her.
  - **Dona Pires**: Carmen copies her quotes but never her ease; the copy is always a little showy.
  - **Albino**: a Portuguese-born speaker, crude; her Spanish-flavoured Portuguese against his European Portuguese.
