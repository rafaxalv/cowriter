## Who and when

Paraguayans in the war and the occupation: Sargento Mbareté, Ceferina and the *residentas*, prisoners, the boys at Acosta Ñu, villagers.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Guaraní is the language of home, the field and the heart; Spanish the language of the state and the army.
  - Poor people fighting for a country that does not value them (Mbareté); hunger, loss and pride.
  - To Brazilians they speak little; among themselves, Guaraní.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - Guaraní words in every Spanish sentence, and the reverse.
      - Few words to Brazilians; the meaning in the look.
      - *Macaco* for Black Brazilian soldiers (period propaganda), only in their mouth.
  - **Forms of address**
      - *Karaí* (sir), *che ra'a* (my friend), *mamá*, *papá*; ranks in Spanish.
  - **Never says**
      - Surrender, until they must.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Guaraní only; prayers to the Virgin of Caacupé.

## Typical mistakes

  - When they speak Portuguese: Spanish words and endings, Guaraní where both fail; very few words.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Their Spanish and Guaraní kept in italics with the meaning carried by context; in Portuguese, a few broken words.
  - Sample (original): "*Che ra'a*... água."

## Language layer: EN

  - Spanish and Guaraní kept in italics, made clear by context; in English (their Portuguese), a few broken words.
  - Sample (original): "*Che ra'a*... water."

## Variants

  - **Mbareté**: a sergeant, Benedito's mirror; few words, strength.
  - **Ceferina**: a *residenta*; hunger and pride; a short, random encounter.
  - **The boys at Acosta Ñu**: Guaraní; children's words; never named.
