## Voice rules

1.  **Facts first; feelings by implication.** Let the fact carry the feeling. Do: "She left his house very young." Don't: "Her heart broke when she had to leave."
2.  **The sayings carry the mindset.** Use the common sayings of her world to show how people thought, and let them stand without commentary. Do: "*Moça de família só saía de casa casada*," people said then. Don't: "Society at the time was very patriarchal."
3.  **Only real words in quotation marks.** Quote grandma or the family only with words the family remembers. Anything reconstructed is paraphrase, without quotes. Do: She used to say she was alone. Don't: invent a line for her in quotes.
4.  **Name the gaps.** Say plainly what is not known: the question never asked, the name nobody kept, the lost document. Do: "I never asked the name of the river." Don't: fill the gap with a guess told as fact.
5.  **Opinions about the family, no verdicts.** He can say how he saw people; he does not judge history or the characters. (To confirm with Rafael.) Do: "She could be mean. She had learned it somewhere." Don't: "She was a bad mother."
6.  **Concrete before general.** Prefer one small object or moment to a general statement. Do: the shotgun fired into the sky over a noise on the roof. Don't: "Trauma cascaded down the family tree, as it often happens."
7.  **Dry humour next to sadness, never mocking.** Do: the stolen underwear. Don't: jokes at the disease or at her confusion.
8.  **The present stays present.** No 1800s scenes in this voice. The past enters only as what was told, remembered or found (a saying, a photo, a document).
9.  **End on something concrete, not a declaration.** Do: "I write it down now, because nobody else will." Don't: "This book is my mission to honour my ancestors."
10. **Never name him or the woman from Spain** (shared rule).

  - **Reference paragraph (EN)**:

Her life was hard from the start. In her stories there was always a river, wide and deep, and a canoe she called fragile, which took her across it to school. Then the animals, always in the same order: jaguars, caimans, snakes. We heard the list so many times that we stopped listening, and now I would give a lot to hear it once more. About her father she said less. It was never good between them, and she left his house very young. "*Moça de família só saía de casa casada*," people said then; a girl from a good family left her father's house married, or not at all. She left without marrying. After that came other people's houses, and no one in the family remembers whose. She was alone. I never asked her how old she was.
