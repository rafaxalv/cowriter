## Who and when

How Luzia talks whenever she speaks (ch. 1, 2, Interlude). Her letters (ch. 13) have their own voice, to be built from BASE-LETTERS-DESPERATE (CHAR-LUZIA-LETTERS); they must sound like the same woman. Character Bible: "Luzia" under Benedito's parents.

## Portrait

  - **Yoruba first.** Nagô (Yoruba), brought to Bahia as a girl; her Yoruba name is lost. *(Bible)*
  - **Fluent but African.** Decades in Bahia make her fluent, but with Yoruba rhythm and word order, and Yoruba words for the sacred, healing and feelings. *(Rafael)*
  - **Healer, midwife and singer.** Speaks to the sick and to plants; sings as easily as she talks; her plant knowledge comes with ritual song. *(Bible)*
  - **Proverbs.** Trusts the orixás and luck ("The orixás will open the way"); later learns that luck is not enough ("Deus ajuda quem cedo madruga"). *(Bible)*
  - **Keeps her head down with the masters**, and teaches her son to; silent about the master's nights. *(Bible)*
  - **Free, older (Interlude)**: a woman who counts every coin, calmer, still singing. *(Bible)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a proverb, a Yoruba word for something sacred, and the rhythm of someone half singing.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.
  - **Language**: fluent but African: Yoruba rhythm, order and words in fluent Portuguese.
  - **Figures of speech**: the orixás, plants and healing, the body, the market (she was a *ganhadeira*), songs.
  - **Change over time**: follows her life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes her recognisable**
      - A proverb where others would give an opinion.
      - Speaks to people, plants, the sick and the orixás in the same voice.
      - Moves into song without warning, and out of it.
  - **Figures of speech**: plants and healing, the body, the orixás, the market, water and earth. Samples (original): "A leaf knows which wound it was born for." "Your head chose your road before you were born." "The market pays the one who comes early."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "The orixás will open the way" (ch. 1–2); "*Orí ni baba, Orí ni ìyá*" (the head is father, the head is mother; see research/music/luzia-songs.md), rationed; after the sale and in the Interlude, "*Deus ajuda quem cedo madruga*"; "Atotô" and her songs.
  - **Forms of address**: to her son, *meu filho* and a Yoruba name the reader never hears; to Inácio by name; *sinhô*, *sinhá* to the masters; to the orixás by name (Ogum, Omolu, Oxum).
  - **Manners and tics**: says healing steps aloud while she works (the habit her son inherits); hums under her breath; touches the forehead of whoever she talks to about their fate.
  - **Never says**: what the master does at night; her Yoruba name; that she has given up.
  - **Under pressure**: Yoruba and song; the lament for Inácio is sung, not spoken; with the masters, silence.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Fluent Bahian Portuguese with Yoruba rhythm: short paired sentences, repetition, the verb late at times; Yoruba words for the sacred and for healing (*axé*, *orí*, *ewé*), made clear by context. No phonetic spelling.
  - Samples (original): "Folha sabe pra que ferida nasceu." · "Teu orí escolheu teu caminho, filho. Antes de você." · "Quem chega cedo no mercado, o mercado paga."

## Language layer: EN

  - Fluent English with the same rhythm: short, paired, repeated clauses; Yoruba words kept, in italics; no dialect spelling.
  - Samples (original): "A leaf knows which wound it was born for." · "Your *orí* chose your road, son. Before you did." · "The market pays the early ones."

## Neighbour voices

  - **Benedito**: her sayings live in his mouth, but rationed; she talks and sings freely, he does not.
  - **Inácio**: fewer words than her; she fills the silence.
  - **Mãe Otília**: also sacred speech, but a priestess's authority; Luzia's sacred words are a healer's, everyday.
