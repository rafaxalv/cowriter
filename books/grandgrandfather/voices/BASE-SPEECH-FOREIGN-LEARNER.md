## Who and when

Anyone who speaks the scene's language as a foreigner and has no voice of their own: Ruchla (Yiddish, learning Spanish), Madame Angélique (French in Buenos Aires), the American balloonists (James and Ezra Allen), Major Chodasiewicz, the Uruguayan merchant, the Descalvados agent.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - A learner's speech in the scene's language, shaped by their own: simple, a little wrong, their own words when stuck.
  - Each one's first language decides the mistakes (Yiddish, French, English, Polish, Spanish).

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - A word from their own language in each speech.
      - Simple sentences that say more than their words.
  - **Forms of address**
      - Their own language's (*madame*, *mister*, *pani*).
  - **Never says**
      - (By character.)
  - **Under pressure**
      - Their own language.

## Typical mistakes

  - Word order and false friends from their first language.
  - Wrong genders and missing articles.
  - Their own word for the missing one.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - A learner's Portuguese or Spanish shaped by their first language, in syntax and word choice, never phonetic spelling.
  - Sample (original): "Eu não sabe esta palavra. *Nu*, outra."

## Language layer: EN

  - A learner's English (as the reader hears the scene's language) in syntax and word choice; their own words kept.
  - Sample (original): "I don't know this word. *Nu*, another one."

## Variants

  - **Ruchla**: Jewish girl from Austrian Galicia; Yiddish words; quick, brave, Carmen's closest friend.
  - **Madame Angélique**: French madam; French words, cold courtesy, business.
  - **The Allens and Chodasiewicz**: English and Polish; technical words of the balloon; seen briefly.
