## Who and when

White Brazilian officers, cadets and soldiers in the war, and white men of the army afterwards: Captain Albuquerque, Constantino, Euclides Bastos, Padre Felício (chaplain), Ramon Benites (gaucho cavalryman), and Osório, Caxias and the Count d'Eu as cameos.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Officers: correct, commanding Portuguese; orders, honour, the Empire.
  - Southern cavalrymen (gaúchos): their own regional speech and Spanish words from the border.
  - Racism in casual words, loyalty in deeds, sometimes in the same man.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - Ranks and orders; honour, the Emperor, the flag.
      - Casual racism in a word (*macaco*, *negrada*), only as the standing rules allow and never by the narration.
  - **Forms of address**
      - Ranks; *soldado*; Black soldiers by nickname or insult; *Vossa Excelência* to generals.
  - **Never says**
      - That the Black soldiers fought better; an apology to one.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Shouted orders; prayers (chaplain).

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Correct military Portuguese for officers; gaúcho speech for Benites (*tchê*, Spanish words from the border, to verify for the period).
  - Sample (original): "Ninguém recua. Ninguém."

## Language layer: EN

  - Clipped, formal military English for officers; Benites's border Spanish kept.
  - Sample (original): "No one falls back. No one."

## Variants

  - **Albuquerque**: humiliates and flogs; formal cruelty.
  - **Constantino**: a young cadet; eager, then changed by captivity (tells the Paraguayan side).
  - **Euclides Bastos**: decent but blind; well-meaning, says the wrong thing kindly.
  - **Ramon Benites**: charismatic gaúcho, racist one minute, loyal the next; drinks, gambles.
  - **Padre Felício**: Church Latin, tired gentleness.
