## Who and when

How Bastião talks whenever he speaks (ch. 2, 3, Interlude, 12–17). Character Bible: "Bastião".

## Portrait

  - **Recôncavo-born, enslaved on the same estate**, two or three years older than Benedito; cannot read. *(Bible)*
  - **Loud storyteller.** Joker, storyteller, capoeirista; tells everything as a story, with exaggeration; jokes are his weapons and his kindness. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **Nicknames.** "The milk brother" (ch. 2), "the coffee brothers" (ch. 3). *(Bible)*
  - **Kalunga's student**: capoeira songs, sayings and the teacher's way of joking. *(Bible)*
  - **After Kalunga's death** he lives alone in the woods; the joker stays, with fewer words and a sadness under it. *(Bible, Rafael)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a story or an exaggeration, a nickname, and a laugh at his own joke.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.
  - **Language**: plain Recôncavo speech; capoeira songs and sayings.
  - **Figures of speech**: the roda, the woods, animals, food, women, Kalunga's legends.
  - **Change over time**: follows his life; changes persist.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes him recognisable**
      - Turns everything into a story, and the story gets bigger each time.
      - Nicknames for everyone.
      - Laughs at his own joke before anyone else.
  - **Figures of speech**: the roda and capoeira, animals of the woods, food, women, Kalunga's legends. Samples (original): "Kalunga kicked a capitão do mato so hard he landed in Cachoeira on a Sunday." "A man with a full belly fights like a sleeping dog." "The berimbau doesn't lie; men lie."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: "milk brother" (ch. 2); "Does this make us the coffee brothers?" (ch. 3), the joke that runs to the end; lines of capoeira songs.
  - **Forms of address**: to Benedito, "milk brother" as a tease, then by name, then *irmão*; to Kalunga, *Velho* or *mestre*; to whites, *sinhô* with a wink; later, to Benedito the senhor, still by name, never *senhor*.
  - **Manners and tics**: acts out the story with his body; slaps a shoulder; sings a line to end an argument.
  - **Never says**: that he is afraid; Kalunga's death, except as a story told once (ch. 12); no to a friend.
  - **Under pressure**: jokes harder; in a fight, songs and insults; after Kalunga's death, silence where the joke used to be.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Strong Recôncavo popular speech, carried in syntax and word choice (*a gente*, popular agreement, *ói*), never phonetic spelling; capoeira songs in their own language.
  - Samples (original): "Irmão de leite, tu mama até hoje?" · "E aí, ói, o Velho deu um rabo de arraia que o homem foi parar em Cachoeira." · "Agora a gente é o quê? Irmão de café?"

## Language layer: EN

  - Plain, quick, rough English; run-on stories with "and then", "and I swear"; capoeira songs kept in Portuguese; no dialect spelling.
  - Samples (original): "Milk brother, still on the breast?" · "And then, I swear, the old man kicked him all the way to Cachoeira." · "So what are we now? Coffee brothers?"

## Neighbour voices

  - **Benedito**: the opposite on the surface: Bastião loud, Benedito silent; the friendship lives in that gap.
  - **Velho Kalunga**: the teacher's joking is Bastião's model, but Kalunga's jokes carry lessons; Bastião's carry the story.
  - **Firmino**: a fellow trusted man at Liberdade, quieter, a worker; Bastião does the talking for both.
