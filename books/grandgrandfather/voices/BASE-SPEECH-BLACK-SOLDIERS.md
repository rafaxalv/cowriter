## Who and when

Black volunteers and freed men in the Paraguayan War and after: the zouaves of Bahia, Firmino, Damião, Roque Júnior, Gregório, Captain Marcolino, Quirino and Dom Obá (cameos), and later the veterans and peões at Liberdade.

Used directly for these characters' dialogue until one of them gets their own speech voice (then that one wins). A character's individual notes in the Character Bible adjust it.

## Portrait

  - Men from many provinces (Bahia, São Paulo, Pernambuco, Rio) thrown together: regional speech side by side; army words on top.
  - Proud of the uniform and of fighting; bitter about how the army treats them.
  - Camp humour: dark, crude, quick; songs on the march.
  - Some read (Gregório, Dom Obá); most do not.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes them recognisable**
      - Army words (*sargento*, *a linha*, *a marcha*) mixed with home speech.
      - Dark jokes about death and officers.
      - Pride in the zouave uniform, in having fought.
  - **Forms of address**
      - Ranks to officers; nicknames among themselves ("Zuavo", "Sansão"); *meu irmão*.
  - **Never says**
      - What they did in the villages; that they are afraid.
  - **Under pressure**
      - Prayers and curses in the same breath; songs.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Popular Portuguese of several provinces, army words; no phonetic spelling.
  - Sample (original): "Morre-se igual, preto ou branco. Enterra-se diferente."

## Language layer: EN

  - Plain, rough English; army words; no dialect spelling.
  - Sample (original): "We die the same, black or white. They bury us different."

## Variants

  - **Gregório "Doutor"**: free-born tailor, reads, argues politics in long, correct sentences (S2 4, S3 4).
  - **Dom Obá II** (cameo): grand, flowery, African-prince dignity, the Emperor's man.
  - **Roque Júnior**: a paulista; laughs without humour; few words about his father.
  - **Damião**: bitter, violent words; the master's whip in his mouth.
  - **Firmino**: few words, a worker; loyal.
