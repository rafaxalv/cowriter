## Who and when

How the wife of Coronel Pires talks whenever she speaks (ch. 15–16). Character Bible: "The Pires family".

## Portrait

  - **The salon's real engine.** At the teas she leads the talk on what it means to be rich: money is not enough, true wealth is culture, manners, a name. *(Chapters, ch. 15; Rafael)*
  - **French phrases, Gracián and Chesterfield**, sweet cruelty, small corrections that make Carmen feel ignorant. *(Rafael; quotes from research/daily-life/citacoes-salao-pires.md)*
  - **Provocation, not a plan**: she enjoys it; whatever it brings is a bonus. *(Chapters, ch. 15)*
  - **Recognisable in one line**: a compliment with a correction inside, and a quote in another language, translated only when she wants the listener to notice she needed it.

## Author's choices

  - **How distinct**: clearly recognisable.

## Key ideas

  - **What makes her recognisable**
      - A compliment with a correction inside ("So pretty. In Paris they wore it like that two years ago.").
      - A quote in the original, translated only to make the listener feel it.
      - Never raises her voice; never says the cruel thing outright.
  - **Figures of speech**: dress, manners, books, Paris, breeding. Samples (original): "Taste cannot be bought, my dear, only inherited." "*Nace bárbaro el hombre*: Gracián, your countryman. You must know him."
  - **Signature words and phrases**: *ma chère*; Gracián §87 offered to Carmen as "the wisdom of your Spain"; Chesterfield on good breeding, read in French (research/daily-life/citacoes-salao-pires.md).
  - **Forms of address**: to Carmen, *minha querida* or *ma chère*; to Benedito, *o senhor*, rarely and coolly; to her husband, *Coronel* in company.
  - **Manners and tics**: a small smile shared with her husband when Carmen gets something wrong; pours the tea while she corrects.
  - **Never says**: money (except to say it is not enough); their debts; anything directly unkind.
  - **Under pressure**: sweeter and slower; a French phrase.

## Language layer: PT-BR

  - Polished elite Portuguese; French phrases in italics, often a little provincial; quotes in the original language.
  - Samples (original): "Que bonito. Em Paris usava-se assim há dois anos." · "*Ma chère*, a educação é mais necessária do que a ilustração."

## Language layer: EN

  - Graceful, precise English; French kept; quotes in the original.
  - Samples (original): "So pretty. In Paris they wore it like that two years ago." · "Taste cannot be bought, *ma chère*."
