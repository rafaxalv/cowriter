## How to use this bible

Who each character is, in short bullets: role, personality, motives, relationships, the real piece of history they carry, and pointers to the chapters where their events happen. The events themselves, with all their details, are in Chapters. The history is real; the lives are invented.

  - **Neither he nor the woman from Spain is ever named.** Everyone around him calls him something different. The reader never learns his "true" name, just as the family lost it. Working names, for planning only and never in the book text: Benedito (him) and Carmen (the woman from Spain).
  - **Grandma's story is the spine:** born enslaved, freed by the war in Paraguay, wins the lottery, buys farms, "buys" a Spanish girl and marries her, dies, and the sons gamble it all away. Every invented detail fits around these beats.
  - **Real people appear as cameos only.** Invented characters do the plot work; real figures (Rebouças, Dom Obá, Ana Néri, Caxias) pass through. The lists are a menu, not a cast list.
  - **Grounding:** the Zuavos baianos material comes from the *Dados gerais* source doc (Bento; Kraay); other anchors are marked *to verify* in the fact map at the end.
  - **Ages are working assumptions** so the timeline holds together.
  - **Chance, not morals.** Much of what happens is luck. The book never judges; it shows reasons, fears and hopes.
  - **No heroes, no villains.** Everyone has decent and ugly sides. Racism also runs among Black people: Africans and *crioulos*, *pretos* and *pardos* (see Ezequiel).

**How this file works** (Nov 7, 2026): each character's entry lives in its own file in `characters/`, word for word as it was here; minor characters share a file by group. Each file names the character's speech voice at the top, and each speech voice points back. This file keeps the rules above, the cast by stage with links, and the Continuity record. To work on a character, read this index and that character's file only.

### The war: Paraguay, 1865–1870

He goes south with the Zuavos baianos, the all-Black companies of Bahian volunteers, later merged into line battalions. Violence runs both ways (Damião; Albuquerque, Benites). On the battlefield the field is level; in the camp it is not.

- [Real figures (cameos)](war-real-figures.md): Quirino, Marcolino, Dom Obá, Rebouças, Osório, Caxias, Ana Néri, the Count d'Eu

## Who is around at each stage (a menu, not a cast list)

  - **Bahia (1846–1865):** Luzia, Inácio, Coronel Menezes, Dona Mariana, Tonico, Bastião, Velho Kalunga, Rosária, Ezequiel, Mãe Otília. Mentioned: the Malê rebels, the 1857 *ganhadores* strike.
  - **War (1865–1870):** Quirino, Roque Júnior, Marcolino, Dom Obá, Gregório, Damião, Ramon Benites, Padre Felício, Constantino, Mbareté, Ceferina, the boy at Acosta Ñu, Captain Albuquerque. Passing through: Rebouças, Osório, Caxias, Ana Néri, the Count d'Eu, López.
  - **Free, not equal (1870–c. 1877):** Seu Bento, Euclides Bastos, Seu Albino (as his boss), Paraguayan workers, the clerks and police of Corumbá; far away, Tonico and Luzia (Interlude).
  - **Her road (1858–1877):** Rosa, Manuel and Xosé Lousada, Don Faustino, Alvarado, Ruchla, Doña Engracia, Capitán Iriarte, Madame Angélique, Seu Albino, Lucília.
  - **Fortune (c. 1877–1889):** Firmino and the other veterans at Liberdade, Bastião, the Pires sons and Otávio, Seu Bento, the lottery agent, Dr. Teles, the Pires family, Lucília; the Emperor and Princess Isabel only as news.
  - **The fall (1890s):** the sons, the Pires family, card tables; grandma's line.
