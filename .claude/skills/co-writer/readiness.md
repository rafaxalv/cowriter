# Readiness score

How ready a chapter's entry in **Chapters** is to be turned into scenes by **scene-builder**: does it hold enough material for good scenes, so that the prose can be good? One lean line at the top of each chapter entry, right after **When / where** and before **Stories**:

    - Readiness: 64/100 · beats 4 · arc 3 · links 4 · facts 3 · decisions 2 · voice 2 (Nov 2, 2026)

It is a planning gauge, not a judgement of the story. It never goes into the manuscript.

## When it is (re)calculated

- **Every time a chapter entry in Chapters changes** (any skill, any change: a new beat, a fact checked, a decision made), recalculate that chapter's line in the same change set, with the date. Also recalculate a chapter when its voice changes, or when a change elsewhere settles or reopens one of its open items.
- When the line is first introduced to a book, calculate it for every chapter.
- The Manuscript Desk shows the line read-only; syncing the Desk back to the repo keeps the repo's value.

## The six parameters

Each is scored 0–5 by reading the chapter entry (and, for voice, the Voice Index and the voice file). Be strict and consistent across chapters: compare with chapters already scored.

| Key | Weight | What it measures | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **beats** | 25 | The key events are broken into concrete, ordered beats: who does what, where, in what order, and how each key moment turns. | One summary line per event | The main events are clear, the turning moments only sketched | Every key moment in numbered beats, with actions and the turn |
| **arc** | 20 | What the point-of-view character wants in the chapter, what stands in the way, and what the chapter changes or leaves in them (a feeling, a wound, a decision). | Nothing about wants or change | Wants or change named for the main moment | Wants, obstacle and the change are clear for every main moment |
| **links** | 10 | Set-ups and payoffs: what the chapter plants for later and pays back from earlier, with chapter cross-references; repeated lines and objects. | None | A few, without cross-references | Clear set-ups and payoffs, cross-referenced both ways |
| **facts** | 15 | The real-world facts the chapter relies on carry truth labels and sources; few items still "to verify". | Real events with no sources | Main facts sourced, several to verify | Facts labelled and sourced; nothing important to verify |
| **decisions** | 15 | Open items: options, "to decide", "to confirm", placeholders. Fewer open items, higher score. | Many open options on core events | Some open items on secondary points | Nothing open, or only deliberate, marked choices |
| **voice** | 15 | The voice is chosen, and it has the profile scene-builder needs: narrative technique (19–26) and scene architecture (27–31) scored in the voice file. Point of view clear. | No voice chosen | Voice chosen, scene profile missing | Voice chosen with a full scene profile, point of view clear |

**Score** = the sum over the six parameters of weight × (score ÷ 5), rounded to a whole number. Example: beats 4, arc 3, links 4, facts 3, decisions 2, voice 2 → 20 + 12 + 8 + 9 + 6 + 6 = 61.

## Reading the score

- **80–100**: ready for scene-builder.
- **60–79**: buildable; scene-builder first asks a short round of questions on the weakest parameters.
- **Below 60**: plan more before building scenes: run the **chapter-revision** skill.

Notes:
- Short chapters are not penalised for being short: a bridge chapter can score 5 on beats with three well-defined beats.
- Prologue, epilogue and frame passages are scored the same way, against what they need (often few facts: score facts 5 when none are needed).
- Do not raise a score for detail that is not in the chapter entry (the Character Bible or the chat do not count).
